# Curriculum review: 13 Nov 2026

**What was reviewed:** curriculum v1.9.0. That is Sections 1–6 (55 units, about 146 lessons and 1,050 words and phrases) plus the Letters tab.

**How:**
- The app's own code generated every question in every lesson, the same way you'd see them. That came to more than 3,000 questions, all answered as a learner would.
- Automatic checks looked for repeats, questions with two right answers, words used before they're taught, word-bank size, audio coverage and ⚑ items.
- A few lessons, the daily test and the Letters tab were played through on the real phone-sized screens.
- Three reviewers read every lesson as a beginner adult learner of Igbo heritage, studying 10–15 minutes a day.

**Nothing has been changed yet.** The detailed per-unit notes are in Appendices A–E.

## The 8 biggest problems

| # | Problem | Evidence | Where it hurts most |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Section 1 is too long and in the wrong order** | 29 lessons. It opens with 5 greetings lessons (38 items). "Boy/girl" first appears in lesson 9, and "I am / you are" is never taught as a pattern. Eating Out, Market, Travel and School use food, numbers and verbs that Section 2 teaches later (e.g. 400/500/3,000 come before 1–5). | First impression |
| 2 | **Lessons are long and repetitive** | Every lesson is 13–26 questions (median 21) for only 4–9 items. Some are 9 near-identical questions on one pattern (e.g. `num-11-19`). | Every day |
| 3 | **Questions with two right answers** | About 5% of multiple-choice questions (150 of 3,031) show a "wrong" option that means the same thing. 13 English meanings point to different Igbo, e.g. *goodbye* (Ka ọ dị / Ka ọmesịa), *thank you* (Daalụ / Ị meela), *went* (gara / jere), *you* (gị / unu), *car* (moto / ụgbọala), *small* (nta / obere). Typing the other correct form gets marked wrong. | Frustration, feels unfair |
| 4 | **Sentences use words you haven't learned** | Of 516 sentences, 25% use a word that's only taught later and 48% use a word never taught on its own (often verb forms such as *asụ*, *eme*). The past, future and clock "-talk" lessons switch to dictionary example sentences full of new words. | Sections 3–5 |
| 5 | **Word-bank and typing questions are too big, or give the answer away** | 18 sentences need 9–15 tiles. In 203 of 425 capitalised sentences, the first tile is obvious because it's the only capital. 177 decoys differ only by a dot (i/ị), which is unfair in a word bank. 75 typing answers are longer than 6 words. 186 prompts contain "/" (two meanings). | Sections 2–5, proverbs |
| 6 | **Too many unchecked (⚑) items** | 32% of all items. 35 lessons are over half ⚑, and 5 are 100% ⚑ (`alpha-tones`, `job-talk`, `plan-invite`, `mem-young`, `hope-plans`). Section 5 is 52% ⚑. | Trust in the content |
| 7 | **Feedback doesn't help you learn from mistakes** | The results screen says "Good effort!" but never shows what you got wrong. A wrong answer only shows "Not quite" plus the answer, with no reason or tip. The question counter jumps (9/21 → 9/22 → 17/20). | Every lesson |
| 8 | **Higher levels don't feel harder, just longer** | B1 (Section 5) uses the same question types as A1, only with longer sentences. Section 6 is mostly A1/A2 material: `pol-please` re-teaches *biko*, *Biko, gbaghara m* and *Sị ya ndo*. There are no dialogues, listening-only questions or stories. | Sections 5–6 |

Smaller things found:
- The Letters "Practise" quiz shows the letter in its answer options ("vowel 'a'…"), so you can pass without listening.
- Six lessons have no audio at all: `fam-sentences`, `eat-order`, `eat-buka`, `mkt-talk`, `every-celebrate`, `mem-young`. Audio coverage is 63% overall, and 55% in Sections 1 and 5.
- The home screen's last line reads "Then Sections 8–8: B2–B2".
- `q-words` starts with full sentences before teaching *onye / gịnị / olee / ole* on their own.
- Proverbs of 9–12 words are drilled as word banks.
- Several lessons cover the same ground twice. For example, `every-questions` and `every-places` overlap with Section 2, `body-health` with the doctor lessons, and there are duplicate weather, cooking, chat and cities lessons.

## Recommended changes, grouped

### A. App fixes: no Igbo content changes, low risk (could be done before the weekend)
1. **Accept every correct answer.** If two items share an English meaning, accept either answer when typing. Never show a "wrong" option that means the same thing. Where they really differ, make the English clearer (e.g. *you (one person)* / *you (plural)*). *Medium effort.*
2. **Cap word banks at 8 tiles and 6 typed words.** Longer sentences become "choose the meaning" or listen-only questions. Remove the capital-letter giveaway (lower-case all tiles, or capitalise the decoys too). Use dotted-twin decoys only in typing practice. *Small–medium.*
3. **Results screen shows the words you missed,** with a "Practise these" button and a "Next lesson unlocked" line. Wrong-answer feedback shows the item's note or tip. *Small.*
4. **Shorter lessons.** Aim for about 12–15 questions: fewer intro cards for items you've already seen, and no repeated pattern drills. *Small–medium.*
5. **Small bugs:** the question counter jumping, the "Sections 8–8" text, and the Letters practise quiz becoming "which letter do you hear?". *Small.*

### B. Restructure: moves units and lessons, keeps every lesson ID so progress is safe
6. **Rebuild Section 1 to about 12–14 lessons:**
   - single words (Ndewo, Kedụ, Daalụ, Ee, Mba)
   - "How are you?"
   - welcome and goodbye
   - boy / girl / man / woman
   - I am / you are / he or she is
   - my family
   - name and where you're from

   Move **Eating Out, At the Market, Getting Around and At School** after Food and Numbers in Section 2.
7. **Reorder Section 4:** practical units first (Discussing, Plans, Doctor, Weather, Cooking), then culture (Customs, Life Events, Beliefs, Proverbs). **Reorder Section 5:** concrete topics before abstract ones.
8. **Merge or trim overlapping lessons, about 8–10 in total:**
   - numbers go from 6 lessons to 4
   - `body-health` combines with `doc-sick` and `doc-care`
   - the duplicate weather, cooking, chat and cities lessons merge
   - `exam-ule` and `exam-uni` merge
   - the thin `media-film`, `tech-phone` and `rel-sorry` lessons get trimmed or merged
   - weekdays move into Time & Days
9. **Grammar units become real pattern drills** (past, future, clock, questions, comparing). Teach the pattern with short, already-known words first. The current "dictionary sentences" move to a later reading or review lesson. Add the negative forms the tips promise (e.g. *Agaghị m*).
10. **Proverbs:** drill only the short ones (3–7 words) with "What does it mean?" and "Complete the proverb" questions. The long ones become listen-and-read cards.
11. **Rewrite Section 6** so it's genuinely new and B1-level. Cut the repeats of Section 1 and 2 phrases.

### C. Needs your parent: the weekend session
12. **Work through the ⚑ list,** starting with the lessons that are 100% or more than half ⚑. Until they're checked, those lessons could be hidden or marked "being checked".
13. **Settle the contradictions and spelling twins:**
    - *A bụ m* / *Abụ m* / *M bụ*; *aga m* / *A ga m* / *M ga-*
    - *anyasị* / *mgbede* / *uhuluchi* (night vs evening)
    - *udu mmiri* vs *oge mmiri ozuzo*; *Owere* / *Owerri*; *nkwụ* / *nkwu*; *Ekeresimesi* / *Ekeresimeesi*
    - *ehi* vs *efi*; *mma* (knife vs good)
    - three ways of saying "I have a headache"
    - Nri a tọrọ ụtọ vs Nri a dị ụtọ
    - the `fam-extended` tip says Igbo has no single word for cousin, aunt or uncle, but `fam-more` teaches *okene / dede / anti*
14. **Record audio** for the six lessons that have none. The new `audio/parent/` folder takes the files.

### D. Bigger features for later
15. Listening questions ("type what you hear" / "tap what you hear") and **short dialogue or story lessons**, so higher sections feel different.
16. "Hard words" practice drawn from your mistakes across all lessons, beyond the daily test.

Headline numbers

## SECTION 1 - the first impression

### Does it now start like Duolingo (greetings, boy/girl, I am / you are)?
Greetings: yes (v1.8.0 put them first). Boy/girl and I am / you are: NO.
- Lesson 1 `greet-hello` has 7 items, 4 of them from one family: Kedụ (Hi / How are you?), Kedụ ka ị mere? (How are you doing?), Ọ dị mma (It is fine / I'm fine), Adị m mma (I am fine). The learner is asked to tell "I am fine" from "It is fine / I'm fine" and "Hi / How are you? (casual)" from "How are you doing?" on day one. Playthrough confirmed it is confusing. The 3rd item (Kedụ ka ị mere?) has no audio.
- Lessons 1-5 are all greetings (38 items, ~110 exercises) before anything else. Greetings 3-5 are occasion phrases (Good luck, Enjoy your meal, Get well soon, Have a nice day, Welcome to our village) that a beginner never needs in week 1.
- "I am / you are" is never taught as a pattern. The only copula lessons are `intro-name` (Aha m bụ Ada, A bụ m onye Igbo, in lesson 7 of the section) and the pronouns lesson, which teaches m / gị / ya / anyị / unu / ha with no "bụ".
- Boy/girl: nwoke (man), nwaanyị (woman), onye (person) appear in `intro-age` (lesson 9, mixed with two age sentences). nwa nwoke (son/boy), nwa nwaanyị (daughter/girl) are in `fam-extended` (lesson 14). A learner reaches "boy / girl" about 40 minutes in.
- Numbers, food, places and verbs used in S1 sentences are all taught in S2 (see "Progression"). S1 is a phrasebook, not a foundation.

### What it should be (Duolingo Intro feel: 8-12 short lessons, 4-6 items each, words first, tiny sentences)
Proposed S1 order. Everything uses existing items/ids; only the stage `units` list and a few lesson splits change.
1. Hello, thanks, yes, no: Ndewo, Kedụ, Daalụ, Ee, Mba (+ Biko, Ndo). All single words, all with audio. (from greet-hello, greet-polite)
2. How are you?: Kedụ ka ị mere? / Adị m mma / Ọ dị mma / And you? (from greet-hello + greet-welcome)
3. Welcome and goodbye: Nnọọ, Ka ọ dị (pick ONE goodbye), Ka chi foo, Ụtụtụ ọma/Ị bọọla chi? (greet-times, trimmed)
4. Boy, girl, man, woman: nwoke, nwaanyị, nwa nwoke, nwa nwaanyị, onye, enyi (from intro-age + fam-extended). NEW lesson, no new content needed.
5. I am / you are: Abụ m ..., Ị bụ ...?, Ọ bụ ... with nwoke/nwaanyị/onye Igbo (needs parent: one new lesson built from existing patterns; A bụ m onye Igbo, Ọ bụ onye Igbo, Ị bụ onye Igbo? already exist in the data).
6. I, you, we, they (intro-pronouns, after the pattern is felt).
7. My name is...: aha, Kedụ aha gị?, Aha m bụ Ada, Nice to meet you.
8. Where are you from? merged intro-from + the nationality items from ppl-talk.
9. Survival kit: Aghọtara m, Aghọtaghị m, Amaghị m, Biko kwuo ya ọzọ, Ana m amụ Igbo (merge intro-learning + intro-speak-to-me, 5-6 items).
10-12. Family: fam-core, fam-extended (minus boy/girl), fam-sentences (3 items).
That is 12 lessons, about 60 items, about 50 min. The other four units (Eating Out, Market, School, Travel) become the start of Section 2, placed after the vocabulary they need (see Top 10 #1).

### Unit blocks, Section 1

**UNIT Greetings (5 lessons, 38 items)** - Verdict: TRIM 5 -> 3, REORDER.
- `greet-hello` (7): see above. Fix: first lesson = Ndewo, Kedụ, Daalụ, Ee, Mba. Bank "It is fine / I'm fine" (6 tiles: mma Mba ị dị o Ọ) uses decoys "o" and "ị" that are spelling traps. The en->ig and ig->en pairs Ndewo/"Hello, how are you?" and Daalụ/"I'm fine, thank you" are look-alikes (auto section 3). Gloss "It is fine / I'm fine" is the "/" problem; use "It's fine".
- `greet-times` (8): two goodbyes (Ka ọ dị, Ka ọmesịa) both glossed "Goodbye" -> en->ig "Goodbye" has two right answers (auto: 6 hits). "Ka ọ dị echi" uses echi (taught in S3). Ehihie ọma / Mgbede ọma are, per its own tip, modern calques; the learner is told "traditional is Ndeewo". Keep Ị bọọla chi?, Ka chi foo, one goodbye; mark the modern ones as bonus or cut. 2 no-audio items. No Ụtụtụ ọma/"Good morning" duplicate: "Good morning" has two different Igbo answers (Ụtụtụ ọma vs Ị bọọla chi?).
- `greet-polite` (8): Ee, Mba, Biko, Ndo are the most useful words in the whole section and are in lesson 3. Move to lesson 1-2. Chineke gọzie gị (no audio) and Ka Chineke nọnyere gị (⚑, no audio) are religious blessings, not "polite words"; move to a later "Faith & blessings" or drop from the Intro. Ọ dị mma appears AGAIN here as "Okay / It's good" (third meaning across lessons: fine / okay / good).
- `greet-thanks` (8): 6 of 8 have no audio. Ị meela vs Daalụ both "Thank you" (en->ig ambiguity). Nwee chi ọma vs Ya gaziere gị both "Good luck" (auto lists 6 clashes). Ijeọma, Rie nke ọma, Gbakee ngwa ngwa, Ị nwaka! are occasion phrases. Keep Ị meela, Nsogbu adịghị, Ị nwaka!, Ijeọma. Cut the rest.
- `greet-welcome` (7): all full sentences, 6 of 7 no audio, 6 "Say this" steps and no typing or bank. Bata n'ime ụlọ uses n'ime and ụlọ (taught in S2). G'ịnwa kwanụ? ⚑ and Ị dịkwa mma? ⚑ ("And you?" has two ⚑ answers). Ndewo, kedụ ka ị mere? repeats lesson 1. Merge the good parts (And you?, Nnọọ, Bata) into the "How are you?" lesson. Verdict: Merge.

## Progression and pacing

Words used before they are taught (from auto section 6 + my reading)
- S1 uses: ana/aga/ga (future, present), bụ, na, ihe, nọ, mere, si, ole, olee, echi, ụlọ, ime (greet-welcome), ji, ego, ọzọ, kwuo, abụọ, iri (age), narị/puku (market), ụtọ, afọ. All taught in S2/S3 or in later S1 lessons.
- S1 teaches full-sentence patterns (Ana m, A bụ m, E si m, Achọrọ m, Enwere m, Nye m, Ọ dị ...) with no pattern lesson. S2 verb-sentences finally explains "Ana m".
- S1 -> S2 inversions: Eating Out before Food (eat-order/eat-buka use mmiri, ofe, anụ, ọkụkọ), Market before Numbers (mkt-money 400/500/3,000), Getting Around before Where-is-it, School before Verbs.
- S2 uses elekere (S3), Dizemba/months, mgbakọ, ọsọọsọ, bọọlụ, ochenkwago/akpa (later in S2), past and future tenses (S3).

Difficulty curve
- Climbs backwards: the hardest typing (8-12 word sentences) shows up in clo-talk (S2), day-night, q-why, trv-talk, home-more, day-work. A1 should top out at 5-6 words.
- Early S1 is easy (single words, single-word MC), then the sentence share jumps (intro-learning 8/8 sentences, intro-from 8/8, sch-talk 8/8, mkt-talk, eat-buka). Words vs sentences: S1 about 40% words, S2 about 55% words; S2's "talk" lessons are 100% dictionary sentences.

Balance
- Too many "/" and bracketed glosses (154 across course) in typing prompts: "Hi / How are you? (casual)", "It is fine / I'm fine", "fish (also back of the body)", "Excuse me (to get attention; lit. 'please wait')".
- Dictionary-quote sentences (Ike jụrụ m ajụjụ, Akụnna nọ n'ime ụlọ, Ajaero bụ nwoke ọma, Ewu na-ata ahịhịa) sound textbook and carry names; they are fine as reading, but they are weaker than the real conversational patterns a diaspora learner wants.
- Grammar is mostly incidental. Missing in S1/S2: "to have" (nwere) as a lesson, negation ("I don't ..."), "this/that" (nke a / nke ahụ) as a lesson, "there is/are", simple plural, "my/your" (m / gị) beyond pronouns, days of the week (S4!), "good morning / night" done, "excuse me" done twice.

Length
- 29 + 38 = 67 lessons, ~21 exercises each = ~1,400 exercises to leave A1. At 12 minutes a day (~20 exercises) that is 70 days; Duolingo's own A1 is about 30-40 units of 5-8 exercises. Per-lesson length is the main reason the first impression is "too long".

## What a learner would find most frustrating
1. The first lesson asks to tell apart "Hi / How are you? (casual)", "How are you doing?", "It is fine / I'm fine", "I am fine".
2. Two Igbo words for the same English (goodbye, thank you, good luck, good morning, you, brother/sister, wrapper, car) and en->ig exercises that accept only one.
3. Typing 8-12 word sentences on a phone (q-why 15 tiles, clo-talk, day-night, trv-talk).
4. Lessons where 5 of 6 items are ⚑ (q-words, job-talk, col-basic, fam-more).
5. No audio on most sentences (S1: 45% of items; eat-order, eat-buka, mkt-talk, fam-sentences 0%).
6. Hearing an item introduced, then re-asked immediately, then in a match and a speak, then in a bank: the same 7 items four times in 6 minutes.

## 1. Overall verdict

- Section 3 mixes (a) vocabulary topics that fit A1 well (time, body, feelings, nature), (b) three grammar units (clock, past, future) too thin to teach the grammar, and (c) "-talk" lessons made of 5-7 unrelated dictionary sentences. The (c) lessons are where it stops feeling like a course: "Ada zụtara ite ọzọ" / "Chikọdị zụtara nwamba ọhụrụ" teach almost nothing that transfers.
- Section 4 is not a step up in difficulty. Vocabulary is more specialised (wedding, harmattan, syringe) but the exercise types are the same. The hardest thing in the section is the proverbs, and that is a text-length problem, not a language-level one.
- Biggest structural problem: grammar is announced in a one-line tip, then drilled only through whole sentences full of words the learner has never seen.
- Second: duplicated or contradictory content (three headache phrasings, three words for evening/night, two words for rainy season, spelling variants of the same word).

## 2. Section 3, unit by unit

### Unit `time` (Time & Days): time-days, time-parts, time-market
- Fits A1 well. The market-day tip (Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ) is the best cultural hook in the section.
- time-days: fine, 6 words. "oge = time" vs "ụbọchị = day" appear as options together; OK.
- time-parts: `afọ` (year) is also `afọ` (stomach, body-limbs) and `Afọ` (3rd market day, time-market): three meanings in three lessons, no remark. Glosses "week (traditional 4-day week)" and "month (also moon)" are also the English prompts in en->ig MC; shorten to "week"/"month" and move the extra to a note.
- time-market: MC options for Eke/Orie/Afọ/Nkwọ include numbers ("1 (one)", "1,000", "100"), which are obviously wrong and add noise. `Ụbọchị Ụka` = Sunday is taught here (⚑, no audio) but the other six weekdays only come in S4 `plan-days`. Sunday alone is odd. `Ụbọchị Ụka` is spoken twice in one lesson.
- Verdict: **Keep**, trim glosses, and **move the weekday names** from plan-days into time-market (rename "Days of the week"; the market days stay as a cultural second half).

### Unit `body`: body-head, body-limbs, body-health
- body-head (8 words): good, simple, has typing steps. Keep.
- body-limbs: "hand / arm", "foot / leg" two-meaning glosses (typing "leg" works only if alts exist; check). MC for `obi` and `afọ` offers "My stomach hurts", a sentence from the NEXT lesson, beside one-word options: easy to dismiss and spoils the next lesson. `olu` spoken twice.
- body-health: opens with full sentences ("Isi na-awa m", "Ahụ adịghị m mma", "Afọ na-eme m") mixed with dọkịta / ọgwụ / Ndo. First three have no audio. The tip explains only "na-awa m". The "my X hurts" idea is not made a pattern (na-awa m vs na-eme m use different verbs, which a beginner reads as inconsistent). Then S4 `doc-sick` teaches "Enwere m isiọwụwa" and "isi mgbu", and `doc-care` teaches "Ahụ na-arịa m" and "afọ ohihi". Three phrasings of headache/stomach ache across three lessons, never compared. Parent: which is the natural first choice?
- Verdict: Keep body-head and body-limbs (fix distractors). **Rewrite** body-health into a "my ___ hurts" pattern lesson and merge with doc-sick.

### Unit `everyday`: every-places, every-questions, every-heart, every-help, every-celebrate
Five lessons that do not belong together.
- every-places: fine words, but junk distractors ("She looks like her mother" for `obodo`, "Where?" for `ahịa`). `obodo` = "town / village / country" (three meanings). Church = `ụka` here but `ụlọ ụka` in S4 bel-god.
- every-questions: 7 "words", 3 of them sentences; 3/7 flagged, 3/7 audio. En->ig MC options are other lessons' phrases ("Pụọ ebe a!", "Jisie ike", "Kwụsị!") so they are easy to dismiss. Tip lists "ole (how many), kedu (how)" that are not items. Duplicates S2 content: auto-issues lists "Where is it?" taught twice (`Olee ebe ọ dị?` in where-talk, `Ebee ka ọ dị?` here), plus `onye`, `Gịnị?`.
- every-heart: nice human content (I love you, well done). But "Ọ dị mma" is now taught in three lessons (greet-hello, greet-polite, here). 5/7 no audio. En->ig prompts "Slowly, slowly / Take it easy" and "Well done / Keep it up (lit. 'hold strength')" give two meanings and one accepted answer. `Ọ dị ka nne ya` (She looks like her mother) and `Chukwu nọ` (God is there) are odd companions.
- every-help: useful survival phrases (Help!, Stop!, Fire!, Where is the toilet?). Good. Minimal pair Nyere m aka! (help me) vs Hapụ m aka! (leave me alone) is a great teaching point but is never pointed out.
- every-celebrate: 0/6 audio. Prompts like "Happy Christmas (Christmas greetings)" repeat themselves, awkward for typing. "Ekene + occasion" is the pattern, but only one bank step builds it. Parent: `Chukwu alụka` (⚑).
- Verdict: **Split**. every-help, every-heart, every-celebrate stay as "Useful phrases". every-places and every-questions **Merge into S2** (or drop as duplicates).

### Unit `clock` (Telling the time): clk-words, clk-talk
First of the grammar units you asked about, and the weakest.
- clk-words tip is good: "elekere = clock / o'clock. Say the number after it: elekere otu = one o'clock." But the items then teach three frames at once: `Ọ nọ elekere otu` (it is), `n'elekere anọ` (at), `Olee ihe elekere nọ?` (what time?). The learner never builds a time. Numbers are from S2 and appear only inside fixed phrases.
- `ọkara` (half) and `nkeji` (minute) are taught as words and never used in a sentence, so "half past" and "ten minutes" cannot be said.
- `anyasị` = "night (sunset to sunrise)" duplicates `abalị` = night (time-parts), and in fut-talk "M ga-eri ji n'anyasị" is glossed "I will eat yam in the evening". Night/evening is now covered by abalị, anyasị, mgbede, and `uhuluchi` (fut-talk). Parent must decide which are real.
- clk-talk has only 4 items (the only lesson under 5 items; 13 exercises): two are the same frame with different numbers ("six o'clock in the morning", "nine o'clock in the morning") that should be built, not memorised. The key phrases are ⚑ and silent.
- Verdict: **Rewrite and merge** into one lesson of about 8 items: elekere, nkeji, ọkara, "Olee ihe elekere nọ?", then a frame drill "Ọ nọ elekere ___" with several numbers and "na ụtụtụ / n'abalị". The pattern is introduced clearly enough in the tip; the drills do not exercise it.

### Unit `past` (The past): past-form, past-talk
- Tip is a good honest one-liner (add -r + same vowel: gaa -> gara, ri -> riri; some linguists say "completed" not "past"). Problems:
  - Items contradict it: `jere` (went, "another word for go") breaks the rule and is ⚑; `gawara` (item 7) is a third form of "went" with no explanation; typed alternative `Agara m ahịa` is a different shape from the shown `M gara ahịa`. Three forms of "went" in lesson 1 (gara, jere, gawara).
  - past-form: 6/7 flagged, 1/7 audio. Typing "went" accepts only gara (playthrough #2); MC offers "went" and "went (another word for 'go')" side by side.
  - past-talk tip says "same -rV pattern: hụ -> hụrụ, zụta -> zụtara", but hụrụ is not "same vowel", and `zụtaara`, `abanyere` carry extra vowels (applicative/other) that nothing explains.
- past-talk: 6 dictionary sentences, each with 2-4 untaught words (ochenkwago, imụlọ, ite, nwamba, ọhụrụ, ozugbo); auto-issues lists it among the worst (5 sentences with never-taught words). Typing "My friend bought me a black bag" (3 unknown words) is unfair.
- Missing: negative past (planned, unconfirmed), time words (ụnyaahụ was just taught but only 1 sentence uses it), any question form.
- Verdict: **Rewrite.** One lesson on 3-4 verbs the learner already has (ri, gaa, hụ, bịa) with short sentences: "Ọ riri nri", "Anyị gara ụlọ akwụkwọ", "Ahụrụ m gị ụnyaahụ". Move the long dictionary sentences later.

### Unit `future` (The future): fut-form, fut-talk
- fut-form is the clearest grammar lesson in the section: tip "Put ga- before the verb: ga-abịa, ga-eri. Aga m = I will. Agaghị m = I will not.", then ga, ga-abịa, ga-eri, ga-ahụ, ga-aga, aga m, izu nabịa. Particle first, then four known verbs: right order.
- Problems:
  - The tip promises `Agaghị m` (negative) but no item or exercise teaches it.
  - Three spellings of "I will" with no explanation: `aga m` (fut-form), `A ga m abịa` and `M ga-abịa` (fut-talk). The typing alternatives for "I will" even include "m ga-" (a fragment with a hyphen).
  - 1/7 audio; `ga-aga` spoken twice.
  - Auto-issues: aga/ga/abịa/eri are used in 7-14 sentences in S1-S2 BEFORE fut-form teaches them (e.g. mkt-talk "Daalụ. Aga m abịa echi").
- fut-talk: goes straight to 6 full sentences and a 10-tile bank ("I will bring you that cloth in the morning": 7 Igbo words + decoys `àkwà`, `ákwà`, `ákwá`, which are tone-mark twins of akwa, so a diacritics test not a grammar test). `Ị ga-enye m ego m taa` (enye, ego m untaught), `ma e mechaa` untaught. Only the ga- pattern transfers, and 4 of 7 sentences wrap it in new words.
- Verdict: fut-form **Keep** (teach `Agaghị m` or remove it from the tip; add audio; pick one "I will" form). fut-talk **Trim** to 4 short sentences with known verbs + echi / taa / n'ụtụtụ; drop the 10-tile bank.

### Unit `feelings`: feel-words, feel-talk
- Happy/sad/angry/tired/afraid. Fits A1, and the tip ("feelings do something to you") is genuinely useful.
- feel-words: 8 items, 23 exercises (longest word lesson). Single words (aṅụrị, mwute, iwe) mixed with sentences using four different frames (na-eme m, anọ m na, na-ewe m, gwụrụ m). No single pattern. 5/8 flagged.
- feel-talk: "Nkịta anaghị atụ m egwu" (Dogs do not scare me; nkịta untaught) is cute but off-topic. "Gịnị na-ewe gị?" is a very useful social phrase (⚑, no audio). MC distractor "I am happy, but I am tired" appears as an option for "happy (lit. sweet heart)", leaking a later sentence.
- Missing: bored, excited, worried (noted in plan).
- Verdict: **Keep**, trim feel-words to 6 items; unify glosses ("I am happy / sad / angry / tired / afraid").

### Unit `people`: pers-words, pers-talk
- pers-words: laughter (ọchị) and "Ọ na-achị ọchị" are not "describing people". `agadị` has a three-way gloss ("old person / adult / old age"). `agbata` ("nearby / neighbourhood / beside") is a bound word, poor as a card; no audio.
- pers-talk: 6 sentences on 5 different constructions: relative clause "Onye m hụrụ bụ agadị", "as tall as" in "Ahụbeghị m onye toro ogologo ka Chukwudị", "E nwere onye na-arụ ụlọ n'agbata nke gị". These look B1, above even the S4 comparison unit.
- Verdict: **Rewrite**: use the S2 adjectives ("He is tall / short / old / young") and move the relative clause and "as tall as" to S4 discuss.

### Unit `chat`: chat-wow, chat-talk
- chat-wow is charming (Hei! Chai! Chineke! Nenu! Inukwa? Eziokwu?), no grammar, 3/6 flagged, but 5/6 have no audio, which is the whole point of the lesson ("say them with feeling"). Parent: is "Chineke!" OK as a casual exclamation for this audience?
- chat-talk: 5 items; `Ka anyị gaa ahịa` (let's go to the market) is a reusable "let's" pattern, never explained here. 4/5 flagged. The 9-tile bank for "My friend, how are you?" has decoys `i`, `a`, `Ee,` (punctuation glued to tiles).
- Verdict: **Merge** into one lesson (~8 items). Audio before release.

### Unit `cities`: city-words, city-talk
- city-words: Enugu, Onitsha, Owerri, Aba, Legọọsị + city/village. Four of seven items are English-spelled city names, so the MC "Enugu -> Enugu (a city in Igboland)" is not language learning. The en->ig answer is the only option that is a bare name, trivially. Real vocabulary: obodo ukwu, ime obodo, Legọọsị.
- city-talk: 6 good short sentences with n' (in): "Ebi m n'Owerri", "Anyị ga-aga Onitsha echi" (reuses future: good). "Obodo anyị buru ibu = Our town (country) is big" gloss ambiguous. Spelling check: Owerri here, `Owere` in disc-agree.
- Verdict: **Trim**: drop the four name intro/checks to one match step; merge with city-talk.

### Unit `nature`: nat-world, nat-talk
- nat-world: sun, star, sky, wind, tree, mountain, river, world: excellent A1 vocabulary with typing. Tip about igwe (sky) vs igwe (iron) is memorable, but tone marks are not shown so the learner cannot see the difference.
- nat-talk: 9 items, 26 exercises (longest in S3): 3 animal words (ugo, agụiyi, anụmanụ) and 6 dictionary sentences ("The sky has brightened today", "The sun did not shine today", "We climbed to the top of that mountain"). Typing "The sky has brightened today" (achakeela unknown) is unfair. Overlaps with S4 weather.
- Verdict: **Keep** nat-world. **Split** nat-talk: animals to the animals unit, 3 weather sentences kept; drop "rịgoro n'elu ugwu ahụ".

### Unit `arts`: art-words, art-talk
- art-words: football, wrestling, music, song, drum, flute, story: fine. `egwu` = "music / song / dance" and `abụ` = "song / hymn" both mean "song": en->ig ambiguity. Almost no sports.
- art-talk: two banks of 11 tiles ("Ị chọrọ ka mụ na gị gbaa egwu?", "Mgba ndị Igbo adịchaghị ka nke ndị Amerịka") and a third of 10 ("Amaghị m etu e si egbu ọja"). These are the hardest items in S3. Remove all three banks.
- Verdict: **Trim** art-talk to 4-5 items, bank max 6 tiles.

### Unit `igboland`: igl-culture
- 9 items, 26 exercises: Ala Igbo, ọjị, ịwa ọjị, Ọjị bụ ndụ, "We offer you kola in the name of God", Iri ji, mmanwụ, "Three masquerades came to the event", "He was crowned a new king". High cultural value, but 5/9 ⚑, most without audio, and too big. `mmanwụ` (item) vs `Mmọnwụ` (sentence) are two spellings of the same word; flag.
- Verdict: **Split** into kola (4-5 items) and festival/masquerade (4 items).

## 3. Section 4, unit by unit

### Unit `ilu` (Proverbs): ilu-intro, ilu-unity, ilu-wisdom
**Are long proverbs workable as exercises? Not as they are built now.**
- What works: ilu-intro (7 items) is solid: 5 vocabulary words (ilu, okwu, amamihe, nkọwa, ndị okenye), a negation sentence with a clear note ("aghọtara m = I understood, aghọtaghị m = I did not"), and ONE short proverb ("Ilu bụ mmanụ e ji eri okwu"). **Keep.**
- What fails: from ilu-unity on, 5-7 proverbs of 5-12 words per lesson are put through the same five exercise types as single words.
  1. Intro card: a 12-word proverb ("Egbe bere, ugo bere; nke sị ibe ya ebela, nku kwaa ya") is the 2nd card of ilu-unity. The tip promises a "word-for-word meaning", but the note only gives the moral ("live and let live"). Learner cannot link any Igbo word to any English word.
  2. MC (Igbo -> English) offers four other proverbs: tests recall of a line seen seconds ago, not understanding.
  3. Word bank: 12-word and 10-word proverbs give up to 15 and 13 tiles, with semicolons and commas glued to words. Not finishable on a phone.
  4. Speak steps on long proverbs (4 in ilu-unity), and 4 of the 5 ilu-unity proverbs have no audio. The learner says a line they have never heard.
  5. En->ig MC in ilu-wisdom shows four long Igbo proverbs as options.
  6. English gloss is the literal translation; the meaning ("live and let live") appears only in the note on the intro card.
- Red flags for the parent: "Ewu nwa ogbenye bụ chi ya" (the note says the two sources translate it differently); "Igwe bụ ike" (igwe also means sky/iron); "Onye ajụjụ anaghị efu ụzọ", "Oge adịghị eche mmadụ" (⚑); ilu-wisdom 6/8 flagged, ilu-unity 4/6 without audio.
- What I would do:
  1. Each proverb gets two questions, not five: (a) "What does it mean?" with three short meanings as options ("Live and let live" / "Patience wins" / "Time does not wait"); (b) "Complete the proverb": first half shown, three possible endings, or one key word missing (osisi / ndidi / isi).
  2. Replace the bank with phrase-level chunks as tiles (for example "Egbe bere" / "ugo bere" / "nke sị ibe ya ebela" / "nku kwaa ya"). Hard cap: 8 tiles on screen.
  3. Drill only the short proverbs fully (Ndidi nwe mmeri 3 words, Igwe bụ ike 3, Oge adịghị eche mmadụ 4, Azụ bụ eze na mmiri 5, Onye ajụjụ anaghị efu ụzọ 5, Otu osisi adịghị eme oke ọhịa 6, Mgbe isi rere, ahụ niile na-arịa 7). The long ones (12, 10, 10, 9 words) become listen-and-read cards only: audio + meaning, no bank, no typing, no MC options.
  4. Show the "Meaning: ..." note on the feedback sheet after a wrong answer.
  5. Audio before text: a proverb without a clip is a poor exercise.
- Placement: it is the first unit of S4. It needs bụ, negatives, ndị: better late in the section.
- Verdict: ilu-intro **Keep**; ilu-unity and ilu-wisdom **Rewrite** (5-6 proverbs in total); unit **Reorder** to the end of S4.

### Unit `discuss` (Discussing & comparing): disc-agree, disc-more, disc-best
Second grammar unit you asked about.
- disc-agree (8 items): the first card is "Ee, aga m aga Owere echi" (Yes, I will go to Owerri tomorrow), unrelated to "True or not true?" (playthrough #9). Then Ọ bụ eziokwu, Ọ bụghị eziokwu, Echeghị m na ọ bụ eziokwu, n'ihi na, "Ahụ adịghị m mma n'ihi na m rịa ọrịa", dịka ọmụmaatụ, ọmụmaatụ. Three jobs (agree/disagree, because, for example) in one lesson. MC "example" vs "for example" are both defensible. 7/8 ⚑, 1/8 audio. Tip says "Ee (yes) and Mba (no)" but Mba has no card. Last step types a 9-word sentence.
- disc-more: tip is clear and correct as a teaching move ("put karịa between the two; describing word first, then karịa, then the other thing"). But the lesson's second card is its hardest sentence ("Ikuku na-ekukarị n'ụgụrụ karịa ọkọchị", harmattan wind, -karị form, untaught words), then five easy sentences. Frames differ: toro ogologo (tip example), ka mma, dị ukwu, dị ọnụ, dị njọ. A learner will not see which frame is general. 5/7 ⚑, 1/7 audio; the 10-tile bank has decoys `kara`, `ka`, `rịa` (spelling traps).
- disc-best: three ideas in one lesson (kacha = superlative; pụrụ iche / dị iche = different; Kedụ nke ka mma?). Contains the **longest bank in both sections**: "Ebee ka e nwere ụlọ mkpụrụ ọgwụ kacha dị nso?" (10 words + 3 decoys = 13 tiles). "Nke a ka mma" vs "Nke a kacha mma" differ by one syllable and are tested in the same MC (good minimal pair, but 6/8 are ⚑). Tip admits "some speakers say kachasị" (parent). The superlative is under-drilled: only two items use kacha.
- Verdict: disc-agree **Rewrite** (drop the Owerri card; move because/for example into S2 linking or a short lesson). disc-more **Keep**, move the harmattan sentence last or to weather, use one frame (dị + adjective + karịa). disc-best **Split**: a kacha lesson with 4 short sentences; dị iche/pụrụ iche go elsewhere.

### Unit `omenala` (Custom & respect): om-respect, om-titles, om-sacred
- The best-judged cultural unit; honest note ("Do not use a title for a real person unless you know it is right").
- om-respect: 3 sentences about respect. Spelling inconsistency: `ọnụ ọma` (item) vs `ọnụọma` ("Emeka enweghị ọnụọma"); `ezi omume` here vs `eziomume` in om-titles.
- om-titles: the first card is an abstract sentence ("Everyone should wear good conduct like a cloth"), not a title. The practical items are Mazị and Daa. "E chiri m ọzọ be nna m" (I was given the Ozo title at my father's place) is typed in full (3 unknown words) and uses an `ọzọ` pun. 5/8 no audio.
- om-sacred: nsọ, arụ, kola proverb ("Onye wetara ọjị, wetara ndụ" is a proverb, so it also belongs in ilu). Short, 18 exercises. Kola is also in S3 igl-culture.
- Verdict: **Keep**. **Reorder** om-titles (Mazị / Daa first; drop the opener and the Ozo typing step).

### Unit `ceremonies`: cer-wedding, cer-birth, cer-funeral
- Good A2 theme. 6-7 items each, mostly sentences without audio.
- cer-wedding: `nkwụ` vs `nkwu` in "Ha gbara nkwu afọ atọ gara aga". "Onye kụrụ aka n'ụzọ?" (Who knocked on the door?) is an ordinary sentence in a wedding lesson (fine as vocabulary reuse). "Eze lụrụ nwunye ọhụrụ" frames it as "a new wife"; parent may prefer neutral.
- cer-birth: `Ekeresimesi` (item) vs `Ekeresimeesi` (sentence), and S3 has `Ekene Ekeresimesi`. `agba Ekeresimeesi` ("celebrate Christmas") verb is a flag.
- cer-funeral: sensitive, handled sensibly (ncheta, olili, ikwa ozu). MC options such as `ọmụmụ` / `ozu` are fine.
- Verdict: **Keep**; fix the spellings. cer-birth and cer-funeral could merge, but I would keep them.

### Unit `beliefs`: bel-god, bel-folk, bel-past
- bel-god (8 items, 23 ex): tip is explicitly neutral ("only teaches the words, not what anyone should believe"). `agbara` has no audio; `ụlọ ụka` is ⚑ and duplicates S3 `ụka`. Typing "God answers prayers said in the Igbo language" (asụsụ, e kpere untaught) is too hard; `chi` spoken twice.
- bel-folk: "Mbe riri nri niile" reuses `riri` from past-form: a nice echo. 4/6 flagged.
- bel-past: 6/7 flagged, 5 without audio; the tip itself says "mostly one or two sources, so check with a native speaker". It is a list of proper nouns (Nri, Eze Nri, Igbo-Ukwu) a learner cannot build on. `akụkọ ihe mere eme` spoken twice.
- Verdict: bel-god **Keep**; bel-folk **Keep**; bel-past **Trim** to 4 items or hold until the parent has checked it.

### Unit `weather`: wea-seasons, wea-today
- Contradiction in wea-seasons: `udu mmiri` = rainy season is the item (typed), but the bank sentence for "This is the rainy season" uses `oge mmiri ozuzo`. Two terms, no note.
- wea-today: 9 items, 26 exercises. Practical (oyi, urukpu, "Mmiri na-ezo", "Olee maka ụbọchị taa?") mixed with literary sentences: "Oke mmiri ozuzo ekweghị anyị lọta" (The heavy downpour did not let us go back) and "Kpakpando apụtala n'urukpu" typed in full. Overlaps with S3 nat-talk.
- Verdict: **Merge** into one lesson (ọkọchị, ụgụrụ, oyi, Mmiri na-ezo, urukpu, Olee maka ụbọchị taa?, Ụbọchị taa dị mma); drop the long sentences.

### Unit `cooking`: cook-dishes, cook-kitchen
- cook-dishes: onugbu, ofe onugbu, ofe nsara, ofe egusi, akpụ + 3 sentences. "Ọ kọrọ onugbu ebe ahụ" (He planted bitter leaf there) is about planting. "Ndị Igbo na-eji akpụ emepụta ụdị nri dị ichiiche" (8 words, 4 untaught). The lesson is all recognition: intros, MC, match and six speak steps, with two repeated items (Ọ kọrọ onugbu, ofe egusi). No typing or bank. Missing basics: ji, ofe oha, ofe akwụ, ose.
- cook-kitchen: sie, garị, abacha, "Kwọọ aka gị" (wash your hands, off-topic but sensible), "Nri ga-eghe na nkeji iri na abụọ" (cross-links nkeji and numbers, but too hard as an MC option). `garị` and `gara` differ by one letter.
- Verdict: **Merge** into one lesson of 8 items: 5 dishes, sie, garị, abacha, plus "Onye siri ofe a?" and "Abacha na-amasị m".

### Unit `doctor`: doc-sick, doc-care
- Practical A2. See body-health: headache is taught three ways. `iba` = "fever / malaria" (two diseases).
- doc-sick (9 items, 26 ex): the tip teaches the reusable frame "Enwere m ___", but the lesson drills "Nwa m nwere ụkwara" (3rd person) and the idiom "Ịba ji ya aka" instead of the frame.

### Unit: memories (mem-words, mem-young) - REWRITE (mem-young), TRIM (mem-words)
Fit: right theme for B1 (past habits, remembering). But the lessons are made of long ⚑ sentences with nothing building up to them.
- mem-words: cheta / chefu are good anchor words. "Achọghị m icheta ihe mere ụbọchị ahụ" (I do not want to remember what happened that day) is a heavy first sentence for B1 and sits at item 3 before the learner has used cheta in a simple sentence. "N'oge ọkpụ, ekwentị adịghị ire" = "Long ago, phones were not on sale": unnatural English and an odd choice (ire = be sold); parent should check it is something a person would say. n'oge ọkpụ (long ago) is a second "long ago" next to n'oge gboo (bel-past) (AI: same English, different Igbo). No audio on n'oge ọkpụ, afọ gara aga (⚑) and the last sentence.
- mem-young: 8 of 8 items ⚑ and 0 of 8 have audio. A learner is shown eight items nobody has confirmed and none can be heard. Two of them (the "mgbe m bụ nwata" and "na-agakarị" phrases) are the useful grammar, then six near-duplicate sentences ("When I was a child, I often went to the market..." vs "...often heard folktales..."), so MC options are all variants of the same sentence and the learner is only comparing the last noun. Typing "The first time I went to school, I still remember it today" is 12 Igbo words (AI: longest bank/typing item in S5). Word bank for the market sentence gives 14 tiles on a phone. The tip itself admits the "have you ever" form is missing ("We still need a confirmed 'have ever' form") and quotes English "Have you ever...?" inside the tip.
- Verdict: mem-words Trim (drop the "ihe mere ụbọchị ahụ" sentence or move it later); mem-young Rewrite once the parent confirms (short sentences first: "Mgbe m bụ nwata" + one verb, then one long one; cap type items at 8 words).

### Unit: hopes (hope-words, hope-plans) - TRIM + SPLIT

"Achọrọ m ịbụ dọkịta" and "Achọrọ m ịghọ dọkịta n'afọ na-abịa abịa" (be vs become) is a good contrast but the tip does not say when to use ịbụ vs ịghọ.

4. Same English for different Igbo ("long ago", "ambulance", "church") so an English-to-Igbo question has two right answers.

1. The quiz gives the answer away. ## PART 4 - WHOLE-APP LEARNER EXPERIENCE

### How a lesson feels end to end
Sequence for a first-time lesson (buildLesson): for each of 6-9 items, an intro card then an MC "What does this mean?" on the same item; then one match round of 5 pairs; then 2 speak steps; then ceil(n/2) mixed review exercises (en-to-Igbo MC, typing, or bank, chosen at random per item). Total 13-26 exercises, median about 21 (AI). Mistakes are added to the end.
- First half is ~14 screens of "see word, tap word's meaning": the intro card shows the answer and the very next screen tests the same item with options drawn from the same unit. Easy, rhythmic, low-effort; but for sentences the learner just re-reads what they were shown ten seconds ago. A better test is to put the other items in between (intro 3, then test 1-3).
- Second half is where real learning happens (match, bank, type, en-to-Igbo) but it is only 3-4 exercises, and the only production type for a given item is random (about half the items never get typed or built).
- Lessons take ~5-8 minutes (playthrough). That fits one lesson per sitting. Repeat runs are 8-12 exercises.

### Lesson length (exercises per lesson)
- First-time: 13-26, median 21 (AI). The 8-9 item lessons are 23-26 exercises, which feels long because the intro/MC pairs happen first. 
- Fewer than 5 items: 1 lesson (clk-talk: 4). More than 9: none.
- Recommendation: cap items at 7, or reduce 8-9-item lessons by splitting; give the MC after intro a "delay" of 2-3 items.

### How mistakes are handled
- Wrong answer adds the same exercise (same item, new shuffled options) to the end of the queue once (R.retried); a second mistake on the retry does not re-queue again. In daily test and practice nothing is re-queued. Counter grows (9/21 to 9/22) which is confusing (playthrough 3).
- grade() lowers the word's strength by 2 and sets due = today, so the weak-word list and next daily test do feel the mistake. Good.
- Gaps: no "lost heart" or penalty, so a learner can guess through; no explanation of why. If you pick "She went to the market" instead of "I went to the market", the app says only "Not quite. Correct answer: X". The retry is the same item with the same prompt, so the learner can memorise the position without knowing why.

### Feedback quality
- Right: a short sheet "Ezigbo!" plus "Igbo means English" and a speaker button. Praise varies. ⚑ note is clear.
- Wrong: "Not quite. Correct answer: X". No what-you-chose line, no hint for near-misses, no word-level breakdown for sentences. Typing: "Almost! Watch the dots" is accepted as correct (counts correct): good for learners but never teaches that the dot changes the meaning.
- Tips only appear once, on the intro card, then vanish; they are not reachable from the results screen or Words tab (check). For tips that hold grammar (e.g. mem-young, pol-please) a "Tip" button on the feedback sheet would help.
- Results screen lists all words of the lesson but not which were missed (playthrough 4); it says "Good effort!" for 60-90%. The learner leaves without knowing what to review.

### Daily test usefulness
- 10 questions drawn from all learned items by SRS score (due first, then weakest); intervals 0, 1, 2, 4, 7, 14 days. Random exercise type per item including bank/type. Good baseline.
- Problems: only 10 questions, so after 50 learned words most are not due and the test keeps picking the same few; a score "7 / 9" for 10 questions (speak skipped not counted); retake allowed and "best counts" which rewards re-trying the same list. Not reviewed: no explanation of what was missed after the test. It does not weight to current Section.
- "Practise weak words" is a separate button and fine, but is 8 questions only.
- Because B1 sentence items have long Igbo, a daily test of ten random items mixes 12-word sentences with one-word items without a difficulty ramp (the feel is lumpy).

### Motivation features present
XP (15 first run, 8 repeat, 10+score daily), daily XP goal selector (10-80), day streak with week row, accuracy % and time on results, bar chart of last 14 tests and 14 days, praise phrases in Igbo, lesson-complete trophy, "Words learned" strength dots (0-5) in Words tab, search and filter chips (all/learned/weak/⚑). Nice touches: ⚑ filter lets the parent review.
Missing: levels or ranks, hearts or lives, streak freeze/repair, achievements, unit completion celebration or "unit test", "next lesson unlocked" message (playthrough 4), per-section progress bar on home, review reminders (no notifications), badge for finishing a Section.

### What Duolingo-style features are missing that would help most (ranked by value to this app)
1. Show missed words on the results screen (S effort): list the items missed first-try (R.firstTry or a missed set) with a tap-to-hear row and a "Practise these" button. Highest value for lowest cost.
2. Explanation on wrong answers (M): store a one-line `why` on confusable pairs (I vs she/he, ịbụ vs ịghọ, aka ekpe vs aka nri) or at least show "You chose: X" next to "Correct answer". Also an optional Tip button on the sheet.
3. Short dialogues / story lessons (L): the app has only isolated sentences. A 4-6 line dialogue (greeting at a market, at a doctor) with "tap the missing word" and "order the lines" would use the vocabulary together and make B1 feel like B1. Parent could write 5 short dialogues that reuse S1-S5 words.
4. Sentence builder from known words (M): "tap the words you know" using 3-4 earlier items per exercise, where the English prompt is the target. The bank currently adds decoys that differ only by a dot (177 of them, AI), which tests spelling not syntax; switch decoys to real other words from the sentence family.
5. Listening exercise (M): "Tap what you hear" (audio-only prompt). 63% audio coverage today (AI) limits it, but all of S6 and parts of S2/S5 would work.
6. Section/Unit checkpoint test (M): after each unit, a 10-question mixed test that unlocks the next unit; gives the sense of levels.
7. Hearts or lives (S-M, optional): not necessary for a family app; skip unless the learner wants stakes.
8. Streak freeze, level badges (S): cheap motivators.
9. Mistake "review at the end" labeled (S): show "Let's retry what you missed" divider so the counter growth is explained.

### Is B1 harder than A1/A2?
Not in a way a learner can feel. Items are longer (65% sentences vs 40-54%) and more of them are ⚑ (52% vs 24-29% in S1-S3), but the lesson template is unchanged: the same intro then 4-option MC, same one match, same 2 speak steps, same 3-4 review tasks, same distractor rules. There is no new exercise type, no new grammar skeleton (negation, past with -rV, future, relative clauses are all just re-quoted in tips), and the early B1 lessons are actually easier at the recognition step because the English options are full sentences, while the real difficulty lies in the long typing/bank items, which are only about 1 or 2 per lesson. The learning curve is flat after Section 1 and then jumps at the unverified sentences. To make B1 feel like B1: use longer typing/bank items more often, include Igbo-to-English reading with 2-line mini-texts, and add dialogue and listening.

# Playthrough notes (real UI, 375x812 phone emulation, fresh storage)

Played: S1 "Hello & how are you" (greet-hello), S2 "Who, what and when" (q-words), S3 "Went, ate, came" (past-form), S4 "True or not true?" (disc-agree), S5 "Remembering" (mem-words), S6 "Please, sorry and permission" (pol-please), the Daily test once, and the Letters tab (browse + "Practise" vowels). About every 3rd-4th answer was wrong on purpose (MC, typed, bank order, one wrong match tap per match round). Speaking step was skipped with "Can't speak now". 1 screenshot used (Letters tab looks clean: big letter, example word, English, sound hint).

## What works well
- Intro card -> immediate "What does this mean?" check is a clear rhythm; tips show on the intro card only when the item has a note.
- Feedback sheet is short and shows the right answer plus a one-line restatement ("Kedụ ka ị mere? means How are you doing?"). Praise varies ("Ezigbo!", "Jisie ike!").
- Wrong answers are re-asked at the end of the lesson (same item, different or same direction), which works as retry.
- Flagged (⚑) items get a "Still being checked with a native speaker" line in the sheet and a small tag on the card. Clear, not scary.
- Letters tab is tidy; the Practise buttons are labelled by group.

## Friction found (in rough order of importance)
1. Look-alike answers in multiple choice. Seen live: "Hi / How are you? (casual)" for Kedụ with the wrong option "Kedụ ka ị mere?" shown as the other choice (and the learner was told Kedụ is the answer, while "How are you doing?" is arguably the same thing). Also "example" vs "for example" (ọmụmaatụ / dịka ọmụmaatụ), "I am fine" vs "It is fine / I'm fine", "went" vs "went (another word for 'go')" (gara/jere), "I went to the market" vs "She / he went to the market". The learner cannot tell which one the app wants.
2. English->Igbo typing/MC has more than one right answer. "went" accepts only gara; typing jere is marked wrong. Same pattern for the two "goodbye", "good morning", "thank you", "you (one)/(plural)" items (see auto-issues.md section 3).
3. Progress counter jumps: "9/21" became "9/22", "17/22" became "17/20" and "19/22" in S6 (mistakes add a question; skipping Speak removes the remaining speak steps). It feels like the lesson length is changing; fine in principle but unexplained.
4. Results screen: "Good effort!" for 62-75% with no list of which items were missed. It lists all words from the lesson but does not highlight the ones missed, and does not say the next lesson is now open. After lesson 1 the next node became "current" quietly.
5. Wrong-answer feedback says only "Not quite. Correct answer: X". It does not say what the learner chose or why (e.g. person: I vs she/he). No hint for near-misses.
6. Word bank: sentences up to 12 words give up to 15 tiles on a phone (S5 "I want to remember the first day I went to school" had 12 tiles; Daily test had 10-tile banks). Tiles carry attached punctuation ("mere?", "Ee,"), and a lower-case decoy "i" sits next to "ị" (dotted/undotted twin), which is a spelling test, not a sentence test. Capitalised first tile gives the start of the sentence away in about half the exercises.
7. Typing step prompt: "Write this in Igbo" with an English gloss that includes brackets or "/" (e.g. "went (another word for 'go')", "It is fine / I'm fine", "long ago (in the far past)"). The learner cannot tell whether to type the bracketed hint or pick one meaning. Hint line "Tone marks are optional. Dots under ị ọ ụ do matter!" is good, but a dot-less answer is accepted ("Almost!" is only an in-sheet note).
8. Lesson 1 of "Who, what and when" (S2) is all questions with no single question words taught (onye, gịnị, olee). The tip explains onye/gịnị/olee but the items are full sentences, 5 of 6 flagged ⚑. A learner is asked to recognise sentences whose words were never introduced.
9. Distractors from other topics or other lengths are easy to eliminate: e.g. in "Went, ate, came" the options included "I saw it on the table in the bedroom" and "Chikodi bought a new cat" for a one-word item; "were" appeared as an Igbo option for "went". In S4, the intro item "Ee, aga m aga Owere echi" is unrelated to the unit title yet is the first thing taught.
10. Home screen: tip text says "every section's first lesson is open" but they are called units on the cards and "Section" for the stage; the footer text "Then Sections 8–8: B2–B2" reads like a bug. Section 7 shows as "Coming next".
11. "New words added to your daily test." is also shown after Letters practice, where nothing is added to the daily test? (the vowels did show up as learned words; check this is intended.)
12. The Daily test said "You scored 7 / 9" for 10 questions (skipped speak is not counted), minor but visible.
13. Letters practice: feedback "i means vowel 'i' - like the ee in 'see'" reads oddly; the intro says "New letter" and the prompt "Which letter is this?" shows the whole description rather than just a sound.
14. Audio: "NATIVE SPEAKER" badge is missing on many sentence items (no clip), then the speaker icon links out to Nkọwa okwu in a new tab. In S6 almost all items had audio; in S2/S5 many sentences did not.

## Lesson length feel
- First-time lessons are 13-26 exercises (median 21): 6-8 items x (intro + check) + match + 2 speak + review. About 5-8 minutes on a phone; the 8-9-item lessons feel long because the intro + check pairs come first, then a second review of the same items.
- Repeat runs are 8-12 exercises.

## Unlock flow
- Each unit's first lesson is open; later lessons unlock only after finishing the previous one. Daily test needs the first finished lesson (message "Finish your first lesson to unlock it" is clear). Daily showed "44 words learned - 2 due for review" after six lessons, so most words are not due until tomorrow; weak-word practise is a separate button.
