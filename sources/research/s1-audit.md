## 2. Possible errors in existing Section 1 items

Nothing has been changed. Each item lists the evidence.

1. **"Biko chere" = "Excuse me (to get attention; lit. 'please wait')"** (intro-speak-to-me). Wikivoyage gives chère as the word for "wait" and "Biko, chetụ" as "Excuse me (getting attention)". Nkọwa has chetụ = "wait a little" and kwụsịtụ = "pause". Suggest the parent checks the spelling chere vs chetụ, and the gloss. Nkọwa has no entry checked for chere.
2. **"Ebee ka i si?"** (intro-name). The letter "i" should be "ị", which the rest of the app uses ("Ị na-asụ", "Ị meela"). Wikivoyage and igboguide write "ị" as well.
3. **"Ọ dị m ụtọ ịhụ gị" = "Nice to meet you"** (intro-name). Sources disagree. ilang gives "Obi dị m ụtọ ịmata onye ị bụ". Wikivoyage gives "Ndêwó" for "Nice to meet you". Omniglot gives "Nnọọ / Daalụ" for "Pleased to meet you". Nkọwa has ụtọ = taste / tasty (checked), but the phrase itself was not checked. Parent to choose one wording.
4. **"ezinaụlọ" = family** (fam-core). Nkọwa has the headword èzinụ̀ụlọ̀ = family, and Wikivoyage has Ezinụlọ. The app's extra "a" looks like a spelling error. Verified on Nkọwa: the spelling without the "a" is correct.
5. **"Ole ụmụnne ị nwere?" = "How many siblings do you have?"** (fam-sentences). Wikivoyage gives ụmụnne as relatives on the mother's side, not siblings. Siblings are nwanne (Nkọwa: nwannē = sibling; relative). Suggest "Ole nwanne ị nwere?" for siblings.
6. **"nne ochie" = grandmother; "nna ochie" = grandfather** (fam-extended). Wikivoyage gives grandmother as "Ńnẹ́ ńnẹ́" (nne nne) and grandfather as "Nna nna/nne". Nkọwa not checked for ochie. Suggest the parent checks which form is usual.
7. **"nwanne nna m" = uncle/aunt (my father's sibling)** (fam-extended). This is a descriptive phrase, not a word. Wikivoyage gives uncle dêdè and aunt dâ/àntí. Low priority; the gloss is correct but may confuse learners.
8. **"Ọ dị mma" appears twice with different glosses.** greet-hello glosses it as "It is fine / I'm fine"; greet-polite glosses it as "Okay / It's good". Nkọwa confirms dị mma = "be good". Wikivoyage has "Ọ dị mma = Ok". Suggest one gloss per phrase. "I am fine" is better taught as "Adị m mma" (also in the app).
9. **"Ka ọ dị" = "Goodbye"** (greet-times). Wikivoyage gives "Bye / see you later". Gloss is OK; the phrase literally means "let it be". Low priority.
10. **"Ị bọọla chi?" = "Good morning (lit. 'did your day dawn?')"** (greet-times). Omniglot glosses it as "How did you wake?". The literal gloss is reasonable. Low priority.
11. **Pronouns: "ya" glossed "him / her / it / his"** (intro-pronouns). Correct as an object or possessive form, but the subject he/she/it (ọ) is missing. This is a gap, not a wrong gloss.
12. **Tone pairs in alpha-tones** (àkwá egg, ákwá crying, ákwà cloth, àkwà bed, ézé tooth, èzè king). Wikivoyage confirms ákwá = crying (in "Ánà m èbé ákwá"). The other pairs are not checked on Nkọwa. Parent to verify the tones before adding.
13. **"vụọ" = carry! (alpha-consonants-2).** Not found in any local source. Wikivoyage gives bùrú = carry. Nkọwa not checked. Verify or replace.
14. **"sh" has no example word** (alpha-digraphs: "(rare letter; used in some dialect words)"). Ask the parent for a standard example, or drop the example line.

Spot-checks that matched Nkọwa (no change needed): Ee = yes (éē, eē); Mba = no (ḿbà); Ndo = sorry (ǹdó); Biko = please (bíkó); Daalụ = thank you (dàálü); nwunye = wife; nwanyị = woman; ọ = he/she/it; ụbọchị = day; ụtọ = taste / tasty; anwụ = sun; oyi = cold; dị mma = be good.

## 3. Alphabet and tone completeness

The alphabet lessons cover all 36 letters in the Wikipedia inventory (wiki-alpha: "36-letter alphabet ... 28 consonants and 8 vowels"):

- **Vowels (8):** a, e, i, ị, o, ọ, u, ụ. All present in alpha-vowels.
- **Consonants (28):** b, ch, d, f, g, gb, gh, gw, h, j, k, kp, kw, l, m, n, nw, ny, ṅ, p, r, s, sh, t, v, w, y, z. All present. Single letters are in alpha-consonants-1 and -2. Digraphs and ṅ are in alpha-digraphs. The Wikipedia source headings confirm CH and GH, along with GB, GW, KP, KW, NW, NY, Ṅ and SH.

**Missing letters: none.** The gaps are in examples and tone, not letters:
- "sh" has no example word (see error 14 above).
- "gh" example is ghọta (understand). Nkọwa not checked.
- Tone is taught only as six minimal pairs in alpha-tones. Tone marks on the other letters are not in the app's data.

## Sources and doubts

Doubts for the parent:
1. Excuse me: chere or chetụ? (error 1)
2. "Nice to meet you": ọ dị m ụtọ ịhụ gị, Ndêwó, or ọ dị m ụtọ ịmata onye ị bụ? (error 3)
3. Family: ezinụlọ (Nkọwa and Wikivoyage) vs ezinaụlọ (app) (error 4)
4. Siblings: ụmụnne (app) vs nwanne (Nkọwa) (error 5)
5. Grandparents: nne ochie / nna ochie vs nne nne / nna nna (error 6)
6. Cousin: okenē (Nkọwa) vs nwa nwanne nwoke/nwanyị (Wikivoyage)
7. Hot: ọkụ (igboguide) vs fire ọkụ (app)
8. Nationality: confirm "onye Naịjịrịa" and "onye Britain" (not in sources)
9. "And you?": g'ịnwa kwanụ? has one source only
