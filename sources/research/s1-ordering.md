# S1 research: ordering food and drink, talking to a waiter or seller

Scope: buka (chop house) and restaurant talk. Checked against `data/curriculum.js` lesson `food-phrases` ("I'm hungry!") and the food lessons `food-basics` and `food-more`. Those already cover: nri, mmiri, ji, ofe, osikapa, anụ, azụ, ọkụkọ, akwa, ose, nnu, mmanụ, mmanya, Achọrọ m mmiri, Achọrọ m iri nri, Nri a tọrọ ụtọ, Bịa rie nri, Afọ ejuola m, Agụụ na-agụ m, Akpịrị na-akpọ m nkụ. Items below are new, or are existing items that need a check.

## 2. Mini-dialogue at a buka

Lines 1–5 use confirmed or sourced items. Line 6 is a constructed combination of checked parts and is marked as such.

1. **Customer:** Biko, nye m mmiri. (Please, give me water.) — IGC
2. **Seller:** Ị chọrọ anụ? (Do you want meat?) — IGC ("Ï chörö anü?")
3. **Customer:** Mba, achọrọ m ofe. (No, I want soup.) — IGC for "Mba"; `ofe` from APP (not in Nkọwa check)
4. **Seller:** Ofe a dị ụtọ. (This soup is delicious.) — constructed from ✅ parts (ụtọ, dị, ofe); the whole line is not in any source.
5. **Customer:** Daalụ. Ego ole ka m ji gị? (Thanks. How much do I owe you?) — WV / IGC
6. **Customer:** Ka m kwụọ ego. (Let me pay the money.) — IGC pattern; `ego` ✅, `kwụọ` ✅. The line is constructed from IGC's "Ka m kwüa otu naira" and is not a verbatim source line.
7. **Seller:** Rie nke ọma. (Enjoy your meal.) — WV

Note: Line 3 uses "Mba" for "no". Nkọwa lists `mbà` only as "nation" and `mbá` as "scolding", so the "no" sense is not confirmed by Nkọwa (see Doubts 9).

## 3. Culture notes

- Thanks: WV calls "daalụ" the most polite thanks. "Imeela" is used when someone brings you a meal (WV), so say it on receiving food. The Nkọwa example for daalụ is casual.
- "Jisie ike" (WV spells it "Jisike") shows support to someone working hard, such as a cook. Say it with an honorific or with nwoke / nwaanyị. The response is "oh!" (WV).
- Cheers: "Ṅụọ nke ọma" (WV). Enjoy your meal: "Rie nke ọma" (WV).
- Money and gifts: WV says "onyinye" is the tip. Practice varies and no source in our set gives a rule for tipping. Parent to confirm before teaching tipping as a norm.
- Price talk: IGF gives phrases for price high, price okay, and "name your price" (kwe ọnụ). This suggests bargaining is normal in markets. No source confirms it for buka or restaurants.

## 4. Doubts for the parent

1. **App line "Nri a tọrọ ụtọ"** (food-phrases) for "This food is delicious": Nkọwa's example is "Nri a dị ụtọ." WV and IGC also use dị. Nkọwa does list tọ ụtọ as "taste, cause pleasure" ("Ọ na-atọ m ụtọ" = it tastes good to me), so tọrọ ụtọ is not impossible. Suggest changing to "Nri a dị ụtọ" unless a native speaker says otherwise.
2. **"Afọ ejuola m"** (I am full): Nkọwa has no entry for ejuola, and ejula means "snail". WV has "Afọ ejula m". The verb for "full" is not confirmed.
3. **"Achọkwuru m"** (I want more): not in Nkọwa. Is there a standard word for "more" in ordering?
4. **"O zuola"** (it's enough): not in Nkọwa. zuru means "gather" or "steal".
5. **Beer, tea, glass:** biye means "imprint" in Nkọwa, so "biye = beer" is not confirmed. ti is not in Nkọwa. kalama is not an entry (karama appears as a related term).
6. **Table:** Nkọwa lists tebulu only as a dialect variant of okpokoro (container). Table = ochenkwago in Nkọwa. Which word do native speakers use in a buka?
7. **Imeela:** appears only as a dialect variant under daalụ. Is "you've done it" its meaning, and is it used for food?
8. **"Ọ dị ụtọ" as "It was delicious":** WV gives this form for both "This food is delicious" and "It was delicious". Is the past expressed by context only, or by another form?
9. **"Mba" as no:** IGC uses "Mba," for no. Nkọwa does not list that sense for mba.
10. **The bill:** "ụgwọ" is confirmed as fee / payment. The phrase "Nye m mgbakọ ego" (WV) and the alternative "Ógwọ, biko" are not checked. Which is used?
11. **Ice and sparkling water:** "ihe oyi" (ice) is not confirmed. WV's "mmiri ọgbụgbọ" (sparkling water) uses ọgbụgbọ, which in WV's own gloss is not obviously about fizz. Nkọwa was not checked for ọgbụgbọ. Is this right?
12. **Drink word:** APP uses "ihe Ọṅụṅụ" as the unit title for food and drink; WV gives "íhyẹ́ ọ́ñụ́ñụ́". Which is the everyday word for "drink" (noun)?
13. **Palm wine:** WV gives "ngwọ" and "nkwu". Nkọwa lists nkwụ as "wine from oil palm tree" and ngwọ only as a related term. Which palm wine word is used in Igboland?
14. **WV tone marks:** WV's diacritics and glosses are not consistent (for example, "achọrọ m iri àgwà" is glossed with "osikapa", and "I want to lick pear" looks like a bad translation). Treat WV spellings as unverified unless Nkọwa confirms them.
15. **Dialect:** no source here says which ordering words differ between Central Igbo, Owerri and Onitsha. All items above are unlabelled for dialect.
