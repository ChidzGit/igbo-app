## 2. Mini-dialogue

Customer: Ego ole ka ihe a bụ? (How much is this?) [useful-phrases; ilanguages]
Seller: Naịra narị ise. (500 naira.) [numbers page; components ✅, compound not checked]
Customer: Ọ dị oke ọnụ. (It's too expensive.) [igbolearn; wikivoyage; ⚠]
Customer: Biko, belata ọnụ ya. (Please reduce the price.) [igbolearn; oboe snippet; ⚠]
Seller: Ọ dị mma, naịra narị anọ. (Okay, 400 naira.) [igbofocus "dị mma"; ⚠]
Customer: Ọ dị mma. Achọrọ m ya. (Fine. I want it.) [achọrọ m: app pattern; ya not checked]

Note: "Achọrọ m ya" means "I want it" here. Nothing confirmed for "I will take it". See Doubts.

## 3. Culture notes

- Market days: the traditional week has four market days (Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ). Each town has its own market day, so trade happens on a fixed rotation (wiki-cal; igbofocus).
- Markets traditionally open early in the morning and close around midday (igbofocus).
- Money is "ego" in everyday speech, and the naira is used in speech with plain numbers ("naira asaa" = seven naira; igboguide). Prices are said as "naira + number" in some sources and "number + naira" in others.
- Bargaining is implied by the phrases: "belata ọnụ ya" (reduce its price) and "ọ dị oke ọnụ" (it's too expensive) are standard in tourist and blog phrasebooks (wikivoyage; igbolearn). Treat as expected, not as confirmed etiquette.
- Tipping: wikivoyage gives "onyinye ego" (lit. gift money). It is a phrase, not a confirmed custom. Check with a speaker before use.

## 4. Doubts for the parent

1. "Too expensive": ọ dị oke ọnụ (igbolearn, wikivoyage) vs o di ọnu (igbofocus, meaning "the price is high") vs nke ahụ dị oke ọnụ (search snippet only). Which one a speaker would use?
2. "Cheap": ọnụ ala (English dictionary snippet, NKENNE snippet) vs ọnụ ànì (wikivoyage). Nkọwa does not confirm either "low". Which is right?
3. "Reduce it / last price": belata ọnụ ya (igbolearn) vs Bèlata ego (wikivoyage, uses "money" instead of "price") vs nwekwuo ọnụ ahịa (nzukomba). Nkọwa confirms belata = reduce, but not the full phrases.
4. "I will take it": igbolearn gives agbara m gị. Nkọwa's page for agbara returned agbàrà (devil bean / deity). Not confirmed. Do not teach agbara m gị.
5. "I don't want it": wikivoyage gives the English line but no Igbo. Nothing found. Leave it out until confirmed.
6. "I can't afford it": wikivoyage gives "E nweghim Í ki golu ya". Parts not checked; the spelling looks garbled. Do not teach.
7. "Customer": no confirmed Igbo word. onye ahịa is confirmed only as "person" + "market" (not as "customer").
8. "Kobo": no Igbo source found in any saved page or search. Leave out.
9. "Change money": gbanwee ego (wikivoyage) only; Nkọwa not checked.
10. Two "how much is this?" forms (ego ole ka ihe a bụ? and ihe a abụ ego ole?) disagree about bụ / dị. The app already has "Ego ole?" which is safe.
11. Blog-only items: Ọ bụ ego kpamkpam? and Ị nwere nke ọzọ? (nzukomba). Sound unusual. Check before use.
12. Number compounds (narị ise, puku atọ, narị anọ) are from one page (converted numbers). Nkọwa confirmed narị and puku but not the compounds.
13. Nkọwa's "puku" entry gives thousand but its example sentence says "one hundred persons". The example is wrong; the meaning is right. Check the app's puku lessons.
14. Wikivoyage romanization is inconsistent (ü, ö, ẹ, tone marks). Use Nkọwa spelling and mark tones only when a source gives them.
