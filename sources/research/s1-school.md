## 2. Mini-dialogue

Two learners and a teacher in a classroom. Lines are built from ✅ items where possible. Lines marked ⚠ are constructed from source patterns.

1. **A** (student): Abụ m nwa akwụkwọ. — I am a student. (⚠ "Abụ m" ✅ on NK; "nwa akwụkwọ" ⚠ WV/IL)
2. **B** (teacher): Ọ dị mma. Abụ m onyenkụzi. — Good. I am a teacher. (✅ NK "onyenkụzi")
3. **A**: Aghọtaghị m. Biko kwuo ya ọzọ. — I don't understand. Please say it again. (already in app)
4. **B**: Ajụjụ ọ bụla? — Any questions? (⚠ constructed from ✅ "ajụjụ")
5. **A**: Eee, m nwere ajụjụ. Gịnị pụtara ya? — Yes, I have a question. What does it mean? (⚠ "Gịnị pụtara" constructed from ✅ "pụta")
6. **B**: Ọ pụtara … — It means … (⚠ pattern from ✅ "pụta")
7. **A**: Ọ dị mma. A ga m agụ akwụkwọ ahụ taata. — Good. I will read that book today. (✅ NK example)

## 3. Culture notes

- Schools in Igboland use many English loanwords in everyday talk: "klaasi" (class), "praimari" / "sekọndırı" (primary / secondary), "pensụl" (pencil), "blakbọd" (blackboard). Learners will hear these alongside Igbo words (FC, IG). Expect code-switching in a classroom.
- A teacher is normally addressed with respect. Igbo speakers usually say "Onye nkụzi" or the name of the role, not just the first name (WV, IG). Use "onyenkụzi" when talking about someone's job. Parent to confirm the polite address form.
- "Asking a question" is ordinary in class. Learners who are unsure are expected to ask the teacher directly, and the app's "Biko kwuo ya ọzọ" (say it again) fits a classroom setting (inference from NK and IG; not stated as a rule in any source).
- Dialect: spellings of school words vary by region and by source (for example "ụlọ akwụkwọ" in WV and "ülö akwükwö" in IG). The Ọnwụ standard used in the app is correct for NK and is the spelling to teach. Parent to confirm the local form used in their state (Imo state numbers are taught in school per Omniglot, sources/converted/numbers-in-igbo-n-g-g.md).

## 4. Doubts for the parent

1. **Pen**: which word do people say? FC gives "mkpịsị akwụkwọ", "ihe eji ede akwụkwọ", "pensụl" (also used for pencil), "bairo", and "edemede" (essay in FC, pen in another list). NK's "mkpịsị" means stick, and NK's "peni" means money or penis, so neither should be used for pen. Please confirm.
2. **Pencil**: "pensụl" (loanword, EL and FC) or "uriakwụkwọ" (FC only)?
3. **Student**: "nwa akwụkwọ" (WV, IL) or "nwata akwụkwọ" (WV, IG)? Both are given.
4. **School and library**: is "ụlọ akwụkwọ" the normal phrase for school? Is "ọba akwụkwọ" (WV) or "ụlọ ana eweta akwụkwọ" (FC) the normal word for library?
5. **I know**: none of the sources give a positive "I know". Is "amara m" right?
6. **What does X mean?**: "Gịnị pụtara …?" (constructed from NK "pụta") versus "Kedụ ihe a n'akpọ okwu a na Bekee?" (IL) versus "Gịnị ka ị chere?" (IL, UP). Which do Igbo speakers use?
7. **Learn and study**: "mụta" (NK noun, "learning") versus "muọ" (FC "learn"). Which verb is used for "I am learning"? "Amụ" appears in IG ("I am studying Biology").
8. **Write**: NK's "de" is the root. What is the infinitive learners should teach, "ide" or "de"?
9. **Homework, exam, class, lesson**: not found in any source as a confirmed word. "Ule/nlele akwụkwọ" (FC) is the only exam term; "ọrụ ụlọ" for homework was not found and is not in this list.
10. **Dialect**: is the school vocabulary the same in Central, Owerri and Onitsha areas?
