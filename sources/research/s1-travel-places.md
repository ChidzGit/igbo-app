## 2. Mini-dialogue

Sources: IGC (Have a Safe Journey) and WV lines, lightly adapted. Igbo lines are from the sources; English is from the sources.

- A: **Olee ebe ị na-aga?** (Where are you going?) [IGC]
- B: **Aga m ama ụzọ gaa n'ụlọ m echi.** (I am going early to my house tomorrow.) [IGC]
- A: **Ọ dị nso?** Is it close from here? (Is it near?) [IGC: "Ö dï nso na ebea?"]
- B: **E-e, ọ dị ezigbo nso.** (Yes, it is very near.) [IGC]
- A: **Biko, buga m ebe ahụ.** (Please take me there.) [IGC: "Biko, buga m Obiöma street"]
- B: **Ụgbọala ka m ga-eji.** (I'll go by car.) [not sourced; built from ụgbọala and WV "Take me to ___"] **Treat this line as unconfirmed.**
- A: **Ijeọma!** (Safe journey!) [NO ijeọma]
- B: **Nnọọ!** (Welcome!) [NO nnọọ, when they return]

Note: the last two lines are the pair a learner would use when someone leaves and when someone comes back.

## 3. Culture notes

- **Greet on arrival and departure.** "Ijeọma" (safe journey) is said when someone leaves; "Nnọọ" (welcome) when someone arrives or visits. Both are sourced on NO and IGC.
- **Ụlọ** means house, and also "the place where one lives permanently" (NO). In a home-village context, "going home" can mean going to one's hometown, not just the current house (NO example: "Anyị ga-ala ụlọ na Dizemba" = we will go home in December).
- **Village vs town.** Obodo is town or country on NO; the word for village/street is ogbe (NO). IGC uses "n'ime obodo" (in the village) for villages in the rural sense, so obodo and ogbe overlap in use.
- **Transport words vary by region.** Keke and okada are Nigerian English loanwords used across the country; neither is confirmed as standard Igbo on NO. Expect many learners to hear these in Lagos or Enugu rather than an Igbo word.
- **Market days.** Igbo communities traditionally have a four-day week with a market day in each town (igbofocus, saved). "Ahịa" for market is fine; the day names are another topic (Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ).

## 4. Doubts for the parent

1. **"ụka" for church.** Curriculum "every-places" lists ụka = church. Nkọwa's "ụka" means speech/talk/trouble (and "sour"). WV and IGC both use **ụlọ ụka** for church. Consider changing the curriculum word to "ụlọ ụka" and checking that phrase on NO first.
2. **Village.** Curriculum lists obodo = town/village/country. NO gives obodo = town/country and ogbe = village/street. IGC uses "n'ime obodo" for "in the village". Decide which word to teach for village.
3. **ụgbọala vs bọsu for bus.** WV gives "ụgbọ ala" for bus and also "bọsu". NO gives ụgbọala = car/truck/any land vehicle. Pick one for bus; NO does not confirm "bus" directly.
4. **Tricycle and motorcycle.** WV gives "igwe" for both bicycle and tricycle, which looks like an error. NO has no entry for keke or okada. Keep keke and okada only if you want Nigerian-English loanwords in the app, marked as such; do not teach "igwe" as tricycle.
5. **Airplane / airport.** WV has ụgbọ elu and ọdụ ugboelu, ILG has ọdụ ụgbọelu (one word). NO has no entry for "ụgbọ elu". Confirm the standard form before teaching.
6. **Safe journey.** NO confirms one-word "ijeọma" with the gloss "safe journey". Two-word "ije ọma" appears in IGC ("jee nke ọma", a wish) and in an unverified search result. Pick the spelling for the app; the dialect meaning is "good journey".
7. **"Dị anya" (far) and "lọta" (come home).** "dị anya" is sourced only in WV, and NO confirms "anya" only as eye. "lọta" is a WV-only word and is not on NO. Both need a native-speaker check.
8. **"Where are you from?"** Two sourced forms (ILG "kedụ ebe ị si?", IGC "olee ebe i si?"). NO's "sí" entry is partly visible, and the "from" sense is not confirmed in the text I could read. The curriculum "Ebee ka i si?" (intro-name) is a third form. Decide on one.
9. **"Where do you live?"** ILG "Kedụ ebe ị bi?" and IGC "Olee ebe i bi" are both sourced. The web-search phrase "ebee ka i bi" is not confirmed.
10. **Preposition for "from/in" place names.** IGC uses "na" (e.g. "ebi m na 6, Obiöma Lane"), ILG uses "bi m Amerika" (no preposition), NO uses n' (e.g. "n'Legọọsị"). Parent should confirm "bi n'Enugụ" vs "bi na Enugụ" for "I live in Enugu". Enugụ itself was not confirmed on NO (the page showed town entries).
11. **Stop.** "Kwụsị" (stop) is in curriculum and WV; not checked on NO. "Kwụsị ebe a" (stop here) is constructed from WV parts and is not an exact sourced sentence.
12. **Hospital.** "ụlọ ọgwụ" (IGC and WV) and "ụlọ ahụ ike" (WV) are both sourced. NO has ọgwụ = medicine, not hospital. Choose one.
