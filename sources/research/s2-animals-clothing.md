## 3. Culture notes

- **Tortoise (mbe) is a trickster.** Folk tales often show the tortoise winning by cleverness rather than strength. The tale "Mbe na Enyi" (Tortoise and Elephant) is a common retelling. Search results (Ma's Garden, folktales.africa) are web retellings, not scholarly collections.
- **Animal names carry meaning and tone.** Several words change meaning with tone: enyi (friend) / ényí (elephant), agụ (leopard) / agụụ (hunger), oke (rat, male, share, boundary). Teaching a tone-blind word can mislead.
- **Wrappers and headties.** A wrapper (ọgọdọ) is a cloth tied round the waist, worn by men and women. A headtie (ịchafụ) is worn on the head or neck for warmth, religious reasons or fashion (NO entry).
- **Cloth words are shared.** akwa (cloth) and akwà (bed) are tone-distinct on NO. Egg is also akwa in the curriculum. Keep the tone pattern in mind.

## 4. Doubts for the parent

1. **Trousers.** BU gives ịba for pants, but Nkọwa gives ịba as fever/malaria and no trousers sense. Nkọwa's closest word is ọpante (pants, knickers, panties, underwear). Which word should the app teach for trousers: ọpante, or another word from a native speaker? Also, is "traụza" (search only) used?
2. **Wrapper.** ọgọdọ (NO: loincloth/waist cloth, Nigerian usage for wrapper) or àbàdá (NO: wax-print wrapper)? Which should the app use?
3. **Shirt.** BU gives uwe/afe as shirt. Nkọwa gives uwe as dress/clothes only. Is there a dedicated shirt word? Is "uwe ahụ" (upper garment, from EZC "uweyi ahụ") correct?
4. **Dress.** BU and EZC say akwa = dress. NO says akwa = cloth/fabric, garment. Is "akwa" the right word for dress, or should "dress" be "uwe"?
5. **Red cap (okpu mmee) and okpu ozo.** Not confirmed in any source here. Is there a standard word for the red ceremonial cap?
6. **Skirt, socks, coat, jacket.** Nkọwa did not load for skeetị or sọkụsụ after 3 attempts each. Loanword spellings are from BU only. Please check with a native speaker or retry Nkọwa.
7. **Monkey (enwe).** Nkọwa did not load after 3 attempts. EZA and IGS agree on enwe; please confirm.
8. **Enyi: friend vs elephant.** The app's existing `enyi` (friend) has no tones. Nkọwa shows enyì = friend and ényí = elephant. Should the friend item carry the tone mark?
9. **Goat: male and female.** mkpi (male) and nne ewu (female) are from WS-A only. Not on NO or EZA/IGS. Please confirm.
10. **"The goat is eating" and "I'm wearing…".** No verified sentence in the sources. Candidate patterns are not confirmed: "Ewu na-eri nri" (goat eats food) and "Ana m eyi …" (I am wearing …). Please provide or confirm a sentence.
11. **"Your shoes are nice."** Not sourced. Please confirm a Igbo form (possible pattern: "Akpụkpụụkwụ gị dị mma").
12. **"oke" tone.** Rat/mouse is óké/òké on NO, but òké is also "a lot" and òkè is "share". Which tone does the app need?
13. **Tiger vs leopard.** NO gives agụ as "leopard; tiger". EZA gives agụ for both leopard and tiger; IGS gives agụ iyi for crocodile. Teach leopard as agụ, and tiger as agụ too, or wait for a native speaker?
14. **Duplicate check.** ọkụkọ (chicken) and azụ (fish) are already in the food unit. Sentence "Ọ bụ ezi" (He is very dirty) is figurative; literal pig sentence is not sourced.
