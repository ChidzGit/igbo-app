## Topic 1: Describing qualities (adjectives and stative verbs)

### How Igbo describes things (summary)

- **True adjectives** (ukwu, nta, ọma, ọjọọ, ọhụrụ, ochie, ogologo, mkpụmkpụ) normally come **after the noun**: "ihe ukwu" = big thing (NK example "Naanị ihe ukwu na-amasị m"); "nwoke ọma" = good man (NK example "Ajaero bụ nwoke ọma"). The search snippets (LS, Leiden abstract) also say adjectives follow the noun. Exceptions to check: ezigbo and ajọ are reported to come **before** the noun (Leiden abstract, unfetched), so "ezigbo mmadụ" is the form to teach.
- **"dị" + adjective** works like "be + adjective": "Ọ dị mma" = it is fine/good (NK confirms dị = be; dị mma = be good, per the s1-audit check). "Ụlọ ahụ buru ibu" (the house is big) uses a stative verb (buru) instead of dị.
- IG says "dị is used with a noun and not adjectives", but IG's own examples use dị with adjectives ("nkea dị mma" = this is good). Treat the IG note as wrong or unclear; the examples are what learners will hear.

**Dialect and variant notes (Topic 1):**
- Big: ukwu (NK, IG, LS) vs nnukwu (ELI; also in app as grandparent term). Both appear in sources.

- Good/bad: ọma / ọjọọ (NK, IG) vs ezigbo / ajọ (ELI, Leiden). Owerri/Onitsha forms not checked.

## Culture notes

- **Adjectives come after the noun**, so "nwoke ọma" (good man) and "ihe ukwu" (big thing) are the normal word order (NK examples). Teach the noun first.
- **"Bekee"** (NK) means the English language and English people, and NK also gives "foreign to Igbo people, especially from overseas". Many speakers use it for white or foreign people in everyday speech; it is worth explaining that it can sound like a label, and that "onye ọcha" (a white person) is the more literal phrase. This is an inference from NK and IG, not a stated rule.
- **Greetings and courtesy**: OMG gives "Ọ di mụ mma" / "A di mụ mma" as the reply to "How are you?", so learners can answer with "Ọ dị mma" or "Adị m mma" (app items). This is from OMG only.

## Doubts for the parent

1. **Hot**: no source gives "hot" as an adjective. NK's "ọkụ" is "profession" and "plate" (noun) and "ọkụ" also means fire. What is the everyday word for hot weather or food?
2. **Good vs bad placement**: ezigbo before the noun (Leiden) and ọma after the noun (NK) — is that right, and does ajọ or ọjọọ come before or after?
3. **Dị + adjective vs buru**: IG says dị is used with nouns, not adjectives, yet its examples use dị with adjectives. Which pattern do speakers use: "Ụlọ ahụ dị ukwu" or "Ụlọ ahụ buru ibu"?
4. **Big / small / old**: ukwu vs nnukwu; nta vs obere vs obele; ochie vs agadi. Which is the everyday word? The app already uses ochie in grandparent terms.
5. **Nigeria**: "Naịjirịa" was not found on NK without dots. Please confirm the spelling, then check "onye Naịjirịa" (also app lines 346–349, unsure).
6. **Britain / Ghana / Africa**: "onye Britain" is unsure. No source for Ghana or Africa was found. Should these be taught, and with which form?
7. **Bekee and onye ọcha**: is "bekee" used for white people in daily speech, and is "onye ọcha" the polite choice?
8. **But**: "mana" (IG) vs "ma" (JOL/UCT). "Ma ọ bụ" (or) and "n'ihi na" (because) are not in any source. Which forms do speakers use?
9. **So / therefore**: NK supports "yabụ" (therefore). "Ya mere" is not on NK. Use yabụ?
10. **Also**: "-kwa" (app: "Ị dịkwa mma?") vs "-kwazi" (IG). Which is correct?
11. **Quickly / slowly / always**: "ọsịịsọ" is not on NK; "nwayọọ" is not on NK (only "nwayọ" = calm). "ngwa ngwa" is in Omniglot only. "mgbe niile" (always) has no source. Please advise.
12. **Again**: NK's first "ọzọ" entries are "ape". Is "ọzọ" = again confirmed by a native speaker?
13. **Now**: NK has "ugbua" (one word). The app uses "ugbu a" (two words). Which spelling should the app use?
