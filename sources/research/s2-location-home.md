## Part 1 mini-dialogue (location)

A: Olee ebe akpa ahụ dị? (Where is the bag?) [IG pattern; akpa ✅ NO]
B: Akpa m nọ n'elu ochenkwago. (My bag is on the table.) [NO example sentence]
A: Ọ dị nso? (Is it near?) [IG / curriculum]
B: E-e, ọ dị ezigbo nso. (Yes, it is very near.) [IG]
A: Biko, buga m ebe ahụ. (Please take me there.) [IG; curriculum, unsure]

Note: line 1 is built from the IG pattern; "akpa ahụ" is the bag. Line 2 uses the NO sentence word-for-word.

## Part 2 mini-dialogue (home)

A: Ebee ka ekwentị m dị? (Where is my phone?) [pattern from curriculum "Ebee ka ... dị?"; ekwentị ✅ NO]
B: Ọ dị n'imụlọ. (It is in the room.) [constructed from imụlọ ✅ NO + dị ⚠ — check verb]
A: Ọ dị n'elu ochenkwago? (Is it on the table?) [constructed from elu ✅ and ochenkwago ✅ NO]
B: Ee, ọ dị n'elu ochenkwago. (Yes, it is on the table.) [constructed, same pattern as NO example "Akpa m nọ n'elu ochenkwago."]

Note: lines 2–4 are constructed from confirmed words, so the sentences are not verbatim sourced. Use nọ instead of dị if the parent prefers the NO example pattern.

## Culture notes

- "Ụlọ" means both "house" and "home", and also the place where one permanently lives or comes from (NO). "Anyị ga-ala ụlọ na Dizemba" (we will go home in December) can mean going to a hometown, not the current house.
- Visitors are brought in with "Bata n'ime ụlọ" (come inside the house) and "Nnọọ" (welcome). These come from NO and IG. Inviting a guest inside is a normal greeting.
- The kitchen word "usekwu" has an old dictionary gloss "woman's abode" (NO pronunciation list). Treat this as a historical gloss, not a statement about current practice.
- "Akwa" changes meaning with tone: cry (ákwá), cloth (ákwà), and in some sources bed. Warn learners before teaching it.

## Doubts for the parent

1. **Toilet:** "mposi" is not on Nkọwa; NO's "mposi" returns "ǹsị" (faeces). "ụlọ mposi" (curriculum, omniglot) is not on NO either. Which toilet word should be taught?
2. **Table:** NO confirms "ochenkwago" (table; furniture for books/writing). EZ-H and WV use "ndokwasa", which NO does not list. Recommend ochenkwago. Confirm?
3. **Window:** EZ-H and WV both say "mbo", but NO's "mbọ" means nail/claw. "windo" is not on NO. Is "mbo" a real window word?
4. **Key:** NO's "ìgòdo" means padlock (example: the padlock of a door). Is "igodo" also the house key?
5. **Bed:** "akwa" on NO is cry or cloth in the visible results. The bed sense is in other results not shown. Confirm "akwa = bed" and the tone.
6. **Lamp:** NO's "ọkụ" first result is "profession". The curriculum gloss "fire" is not visible. Is there a separate word for lamp or light?
7. **Door:** "ụzọ" on NO's first result is "struggle". Is "ụzọ" the door, or only the road/way? Is "ọnụ ụzọ" (doorway) used?
8. **Is located:** Sources use "dị" (ọ dị n'elu, ebee ka ... dị?) and examples on NO use "nọ" (ọ nọ n'elu). NO has no "nọ" headword and its first "dị" result is "di" (husband, different tone). Which verb should the lesson teach for "where is X"? This affects all "where is" sentences.
9. **On top:** EZ-P gives "elu / enu" as variants. Is "n'enu" used in standard speech? WS also says "n'enu akpati" (on top of the box). NO confirms "elu".
10. **Water container:** No source gave a word for a water container or jug. The brief asks for "water container". Is there a word? Please give one from a native speaker before teaching.
11. **Missing on NO:** telivishọn (TV), ụlọ ịsa ahụ (bathroom), ụlọ erimeri (dining room), ụlọ mposi (toilet), ọnụ ụzọ (doorway), windo, tebụl, ndokwasa. Confirm which, if any, to teach.
12. **Outside:** EZ-P gives three forms (n'ezi, n'ihe, n'ilo) with no example. Not checked. Need a source sentence.
13. **Box and mat:** "igbe" is crawl on NO. "ute" (mat) not checked.
14. **Dialect:** WV uses "bedroom: ime ụlọ", NO and EZ-H use "imụlọ". Standard Igbo is the target; confirm imụlọ.
