### Mini-dialogue (question words)

1. A: Kedụ? (How are you?)
2. B: Ọ dị mma. Kedụ ka ị mere? (Fine. How are you?)
3. A: A dị m mma. Ebee ka ị na-aga? (I am fine. Where are you going?)
4. B: Ana m aga ahịa. (I am going to the market.)
5. A: Olee mgbe ị ga-alọta? (When will you come back?) [mgbe + olee: pattern from IG, not verified as "return"]
6. B: Aga m alọta n'abalị. (I will be back at night.) [not sourced; for parent]

Sources: Kedụ, Ọ dị mma (IG, OM); Ebee, ana m aga (IG); Olee mgbe (IG, curriculum).

### Culture notes (question words)

- Greetings often use "Kedụ?" with a set reply "Ọ dị mma" (fine) and "Ndewo" in the day (IG, OM).
- "Ụtụtụ ọma" and similar time greetings are modern literal translations; in traditional use, "Ndewo" or "Daalụ" is used through the day (OM).
- Question words are often said with a rising tone at the end (WB, IG examples).

### Doubts for the parent

- "Olee" (which / how / where) is common in IG, but NO did not give its own entry. Is it a standard form, or a dialect variant of "ole"/"ebee"?
- "Gịnị mere?" (why) and "n'ihi gịnị" (because of what): which is correct for a learner, and does "n'ihi gịnị" sound natural?
- WB: "I bụ onye?" is shown under "What is your name?", but its meaning looks like "who are you?". Which gloss is right?
- "Olee otu i mere?" (OM: "how are you?") vs "Kedụ ka ị mere?": are both standard?
- "Mgbe ole?" (when, what time): which form should the app use, "olee mgbe" or "mgbe ole"?
- Which-question: is "olee" the usual Standard Igbo word for "which", or do speakers use "nke ụfọdụ"?

### Mini-dialogue (opinions)

1. A: Gịnị ka ị chere? (What do you think?)
2. B: Echere m na ọ ga-ezo. (I think it will rain.)
3. A: Ọ dị mma. Ị masịrị ihe a? (It's good. Do you like this?)
4. B: Ee, masịrị m. Ekwenyere m. (Yes, I like it. I agree.)
5. A: Ikekwe. (Maybe.)

Sources for lines: IG (echere m na), NO (masịrị, kwenye, ikekwe), IG (Ö dï mma). Lines 2 and 3 are composed from patterns; the forms are for parent check.

### Culture notes (opinions)

- Indirect speech is common. Saying "Ike kwe" (maybe) or "Ikekwe" is often softer than a flat "no".
- Praise for an idea is often short: "Echiche gị amaka" (your idea is good) (IG).
- When someone gives a view, many speakers respond with "Ee" or "Eh-eh" to show they heard it, not necessarily that they agree (IG, UCLA conversational pattern).

### Doubts for the parent

- "Masịrị m" vs "Ọ masịrị m": which is the usual "I like it" in everyday speech?
- Is "amasịghị m" the usual "I don't like it", or do speakers say "Achọghị m" (I don't want)?
- "Ekwenyere m" vs "Ekwenyeghị m": are these the standard agree/disagree forms? Tone and tense may change them.
- "Ọ dị njọ" (it is bad): is this the usual word for "bad", or is there a more common one?
- "Ọ dị ka…" is in every-heart as "it seems"; is it the right form for "I think"?

### Mini-dialogue (advice)

1. A: Ọ dị m ọsịịsọ! (I am in a hurry!) [WB; NO did not confirm ọsịịsọ]
2. B: Esogbuna. Nwayọọ nwayọọ. (Don't worry. Take it easy.) [Esogbuna: IG; nwayọọ: curriculum, unsure]
3. A: Lèmá kwá! (Look out!) [WB]
4. B: Kwesịrị ị ịlele anya. (You should look carefully.) [frame from NO kwesị; sentence for parent]
5. A: Emela! Chere m. (Don't! Wait for me.) [Emela: unconfirmed; chere: curriculum]

Sources: WB, IG, curriculum, NO. Line 4 is composed from NO kwesị and needs a parent check. Line 5 uses an unconfirmed negative command, so it is only for discussion until checked.

### Culture notes (advice)

- Direct commands can sound harsh. Igbo speakers often soften with "Biko" (please) or add a reason (IG, OM).
- "Esogbuna" (don't worry) is a common reassurance (IG).
- Showing care for a friend's health is common. "Ndo" (sorry) is used when someone trips or is hurt, and "Ọ dị mma" follows when things are fine (WB, IFB).
