## 3. Mini-dialogue

Confirmed or sourced lines are marked. Constructed lines are not.

- **A:** Ụtụtụ ọma. Ị saala chị? (UP greeting; sourced) (Good morning. Did you sleep well?)
- **B:** Ee. M bilie n'ụtụtụ, m sa ahụ, m yiri uwe m. (constructed from bilie, sa ahụ, yi uwe) (Yes. I got up in the morning, I bathed, I put on my clothes.)
- **A:** Ị riela nri ụtụtụ? (IG pattern: "I riela nri ütütü?" = Do you have breakfast?) (Have you eaten breakfast?)
- **B:** E-e, anyị eriela nri ụtụtụ. Ana m aga ọrụ. (IG, plus app line "Ana m aga ọrụ") (Yes, we have had breakfast. I am going to work.)
- **A:** Ị na-arụ ọrụ gịnị? (constructed) (What work do you do?)
- **B:** Abụ m onye ahịa. (constructed; onye ahịa unconfirmed on NK) (I am a trader.)
- **A:** Olee oge ị na-alọta ụlọ? (constructed from lọta and IG "Olee oge ị ga-abịa?") (When do you come home?)
- **B:** Mgbe abalị. (mgbe ✅ NK; abalị in app) (At night.)

## 4. Culture notes

- Morning greetings are part of the routine. UP gives "Ụtụtụ ọma" (good morning) and the greeting "Ị saala chị" for good morning. WV notes that greetings in the morning, afternoon and evening use the time words with "ọma".
- Time of work follows the traditional market-day week (Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ) in many towns. Traditional markets were a main form of work and trade, per the Wikipedia calendar page and Igbofocus (sources/web-pages/web-archive-org-web-2019-…igbofocus….md).
- Many job words are built as "onye" + the job or activity (for example "onye ahịa" = trader, "onye ugbo" = farmer). Sources describe this pattern as the usual way to name a person by role (EZ grammar, search snippet).
- Respect for elders matters at work and at home. WV says younger people should greet elders and use honorifics, especially in villages.
- Spelling differs by source: IG writes "ọlụ" for work and NK writes "ọrụ". Teach "ọrụ" (NK) and note the variant.

## 5. Doubts for the parent

1. **Doctor:** dọkịta (WV, and already in the app) or dibịa (WV also says doctor)? Nkọwa does not list dọkịta, and it defines dibịa as a traditional healer. Which should a learner use for a modern doctor?
2. **Nurse:** nọọsụ (WV, FC), onye uwe ọcha (EZ), or something else?
3. **Farmer:** onye ugbo (WV), onye ubi (EZ grammar), or onye ọlụ ugbo (FC)?
4. **Driver, tailor, police, carpenter, engineer, lawyer:** no Igbo term was confirmed by a dictionary. Are these loanwords (kapinta, njinia) or Igbo terms? What is the everyday word for driver and for police?
5. **Waking up:** is "bilie" the everyday word for getting up or waking up? Is "Ị bọọla chi?" a real greeting ("bọọla" is not on Nkọwa)?
6. **Sleep:** what is the everyday verb for "to sleep"? "dinà" on Nkọwa means lie down. Is "ịra ụra" or another phrase used?
7. **Brush teeth and wash face:** is "ịsa eze" (LD, search snippet) the usual phrase? Is "asacha ihu" used for washing the face?
8. **Cook:** is "isi nri" (WV) the everyday word? Nkọwa has not been checked for it.
9. **Work spelling:** "ọrụ" (NK) or "ọlụ" (IG)? And is "agbasa ọlụ" the usual phrase for finishing work? "agbasa" is not an NK verb entry.
10. **Eat:** the app uses "rie" as a command. Nkọwa has no "rie" entry and gives "eri" in its example. Which form should the app teach?
11. **Home:** the app has "Ana m ala ụlọ" (unsure) and NK gives "lọta" (return home). Which should be taught?
12. **Breakfast:** "azị ụtụtụ" (NK related term) or "nri ụtụtụ" (WV, IG)?
13. **Every day:** what is the usual phrase for "every day" (kwa ụbọchị or another)? Not found in any local source.
