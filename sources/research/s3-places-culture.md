Mini-dialogue (culture):
- A: Ọjị bụ ndụ. (NK) — Kola is life.
- B: Anyị na-enye gị ọjị n'aha Chukwu. (NK) — We offer you kola in the name of God.
- A: Ị ga-egwu mmanwụ? (unsure) — Will you go to the masquerade show?
- B: Ee. Mmanwụ atọ bịara mmemme ahụ. (NO) — Yes. Three masquerades came to the event.

Culture notes:
- The kola nut is shared with guests and offered first to the eldest or most senior person. A prayer or blessing comes before it is broken and eaten (NK, KOLA-S). Kola is the common greeting gift.
- Masquerades (mmanwụ) are performed by men only, and their identities are kept secret (NFY). Do not ask who is behind a mask.
- During the New Yam festival, eating new yam before the festival is seen as an offence against the land (NFY). Dates vary: some sources say August, others August to October.
- Religion changes the kola ritual: Christians say "ekpere ọjị" (prayer over kola) instead of the traditional "igo ọjị" (KOLA-S).

## Doubts for the parent

1. **Sun:** NO has both "anyanwụ" (sunlight, sun) and "anwụ" (the sun). Which one should the app teach first?
2. **Harmattan:** EZ says "uguru"; NAT-S says "Ugulu". Which spelling is right?
3. **Chief:** "eze" is "king" on NO. "ọzọ" is a titled chieftaincy. What is the word for "chief" (e.g. "ọnye nze")? It was not checked.
4. **City / town / village:** "obodo mepere emepe" (city), "obodo ukwu" (urban), "ime obodo" (village / rural) come from one or two lists each. Which terms are right in Onitsha / Owerri / Enugu usage?
5. **Street and house number:** "okporo ámá" is only in one list, and NO's "okpọrọ" means rod or bar. What is the word for street? Is there a word for "house number" or should it be borrowed?
6. **I was born in…:** "Amụrụ m na…" has no source. Please confirm the phrase.
7. **Lagos:** the spelling "Legọọsị" comes from one NO example. Please confirm.
8. **Kola phrases:** "Ọjị bụ ndụ", "Anyị na-enye gị ọjị n'aha Chukwu", "Ọjị gọzie anyị" and "Agwa anaghị emerụ onye kpọrọ ya ọjị" are from one blog (NK). Are they used in your area? Which kola phrase do elders use?
9. **Proverb wording:** "Onye wetara ọjị, o wetara ndụ" appeared only in a search summary. Please confirm the exact wording.
10. **New Yam:** "Iri ji", "Iwa ji" and "Ike ji" are all given. Which is used in your area, and when is the festival (August, or August–October)?
11. **Masquerade spelling:** NO's headword is "mmọnwụ"; "mmanwụ" is the common variant. Which should the app use?
12. **Niger River:** "Orimiri", "Ọshimiri" and "Oshimili" all appear. Which one should the app use?
13. **Rain:** NO's "mmiriozuzo" is the amount of rain on one occasion, not the general word for rain. What is the everyday word for "rain"?
14. **Forest / bush:** "ọhịa" is not on NO. Is "oke ọhịa" the right word for forest?
15. **Animals:** "agụiyi" (crocodile) and "ugo" (eagle) are on NO. "agbịsị" (ant), "aruru" (white ant), "akpolide" (taylor ant) are from blogs only. Please confirm. Hippo and parrot were not found in any source, so they are not listed.
16. **Run and folktale:** no source was found for "run" or "akụkọ ifo". Please advise, or leave out for now.
17. **"gba egwu" vs "gwuo":** "gba egwu" (to play music / dance) is on NO only as a related term. "gwuo" means dig, not play. Please confirm the right verb for "play music".
