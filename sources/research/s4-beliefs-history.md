### Retelling: Tortoise and the birds (English, 7 simple sentences)
Source: [Mbeku (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbeku). The article is uncited, so treat this as a retelling of one version.

1. Long ago, birds carried the tortoise up to a feast in the sky.
2. The tortoise ate all the food.
3. The birds would not let him fly back down.
4. So he fell to the earth.
5. The story says this is how tortoises got the patterns on their shells.
6. Another fable from the same article tells of a tortoise, Mbeku, who tricked Grasshopper (Ukpana) into helping him fake grief.
7. He got food from his kin, broke his promise to share it, and Grasshopper told on him.

Note: the Igbo words used for the tale are only mbe (tortoise) and Ukpana (grasshopper). No Igbo sentences were written for the retelling, because none of them were confirmed in a source.

### Culture notes
- Tortoise tales are told as stories with a lesson. The tortoise is a trickster, and the tales often show the cost of greed or broken promises. Source: [Mbeku (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbeku) and a search summary of academic work on Igbo tortoise tales (not opened in full).
- Folktales were traditionally told at night in moonlight, mainly to children. Source: search summary of the insight.ng article (not opened in full).
- The storyteller's opening and closing lines are not documented in any source checked here.

### History sentences (7 short sentences, with sources)
1. Nri was a religious and political centre in the Igbo heartland of what is now south-eastern Nigeria. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri)
2. Its ruler, the Eze Nri, held ritual and spiritual power, but not military power. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri)
3. Nri grew by ritual agents who won converts and oaths, not by conquest. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri)
4. The start of Nri is dated differently by different authors; a common view places it around the 10th century. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri)
5. Under British colonial rule, troops forced the reigning Eze Nri to give up the ritual power of the ìkénga religion, which ended Nri as a political power. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri) (the exact year of this event was not confirmed)
6. Igbo-Ukwu, about 18 km south of Nri, is known for bronze casting; some of its bronzes date from the 9th century. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri)
7. Nri survives today as a traditional state within Nigeria. [Kingdom of Nri (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri)

### Culture notes
- Many Igbo people today use both Christian and traditional vocabulary. Odinani survives in rural areas, at harvest festivals and in masquerades, and it is also mixed with Christian practice. Source: [Odinani (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odinani).
- Speaking about ancestors, chi and spirits is part of everyday talk for many families. The sources describe these beliefs as varied; no one standard view was found.
- Colonial history is part of the story of Nri and of Igbo churches. Write about it neutrally.

## Doubts for the parent

1. **ụka: church or speech?** Nkọwa gives ụka as "speech; talk; trouble", not church. ụlọ ụka is not in Nkọwa. Which word do you use for church?
2. **Priest.** nchụaja is not in Nkọwa or Odinani. fada is not verified. Which word is the everyday one for a Christian priest? Should dibia stay as the word for a traditional diviner-healer?
3. **Chineke and Chukwu.** Sources say Chineke is used for the Christian God and Chukwu for the high god in traditional belief. A search summary also says early Bible translations used Chukwu for both, and this was not confirmed by a fetched source. Is this how people use them today?
4. **Ancestors.** ndichie is not in Nkọwa. What is the everyday word for ancestors? Also, is ndị nna nna anyị the usual phrase?
5. **Chi Ukwu.** Is this a spelling variant of Chukwu, or a different word?
6. **Chidi and Chukwuemeka.** The meanings were not sourced in this run. What do these names mean in your family?
7. **Story opener.** Is "Otu mgbe" used as the opening line of a folktale? No fetched source gives it. What do storytellers say?
8. **Moonlight tales.** Is egwu ọnwa the common term? The only source is a secondary article that was not opened in full.
9. **Eze Nri dates.** Sources give the start of Nri as around the 10th century, 1043, about 1225, or the 1500s. Which should we teach, or should we say "date uncertain"?
10. **Igbo-Ukwu and Nri.** Sources say the link between the bronzes and Nri is debated. Should the lesson say they are linked, or only that they are near each other?
11. **mmụọ.** Nkọwa gives a wide range of meanings (spirit, ghost, demon, masquerade). Which should the lesson teach first?
12. **Ala's rank.** Sources differ on whether she is an earth goddess or a feminine earth spirit, and on whether she ranks above Chukwu. Which description should we use?
13. **history.** akụkọ ihe mere eme is not in Nkọwa. What is the usual word for "history"?
14. **Sensitive topics.** The lesson covers ancestors, shrines and deities. Please review the wording before it goes into the app.
