### Culture notes (Discussing)

- Greet and address older people with respect. Wikivoyage/WP: honorifics for people "significantly older" are expected. Disagreeing with an elder directly may be seen as rude. *(WB, WP. How strongly this applies is not confirmed. Parent to check.)*
- "Eziokwu" (truth) and "Ee" (yes) are everyday words in conversation. UCLA shows them used often to respond. *(UCLA.)*
- In UCLA, speakers add "Eheǃ" and "Eziokwu" as short replies while listening. This is normal conversational rhythm. *(UCLA.)*
- Dialect: the UCLA recording's dialect is not stated in the file. Owerri/Onitsha/Central variants may differ. Note in the unit.

## Part B. Comparing

### How comparison works in Igbo (from the sources)

- Igbo has very few adjectives: a closed set of 8, in pairs of opposites (WP: ukwu big / nta small; ọhụrụ new / ochie old; ọma good; njọ bad; ọcha light / oji dark). Other descriptions use stative verbs such as **dị** ("be") and **toro** ("grow").
- Igbo has almost no inflection (WP: "very little inflectional morphology"). There is **no separate comparative form** like English "-er". The comparison is built with words placed around the adjective.

Pattern 1, comparative ("more … than"): **[A] + dị/toro + [adjective] + karị + [B]**.
Pattern 2, superlative ("the most …"): **kacha + [adjective or verb]**. Sourced only with "kacha dị nso" (nearest).
Pattern 3, similarity ("like / the same as"): **[A] + dị ka + [B]**.
Pattern 4, choice ("Which is better?"): **Kedụ nke ka mma?**, *constructed; see Items*.

2. **B:** Ee, ọ dị ukwu karịa. (Yes, it is bigger.) — *NK "ee" [src]*

### Culture notes (Comparing)

- Showing respect when comparing people: use "okenye" (elder) and the honorifics in WB ("Mazi", "Daa") rather than calling an older person "nwa" (child). WB says "nwa" can sound patronising. *(WB.)*
- When asking about prices in a market or hotel, learners often compare quality ("bigger", "cleaner", "cheaper") with short questions: "...ukwu?", "...di ọcha?", "...di ọnu ànì?" *(WB examples.)*

### Doubts for the parent (Comparing)

1. **karịa vs karịrị**: NK gives the root "karị" (be more than). Which form does a speaker use to join two things, as in "Ada is taller than Ike"?
2. **Superlative**: is "kacha" + adjective the normal "the most …" pattern? Is "kachasị" the form for "best"? NK for kachasị was still loading.
3. **Same / like**: is "dị ka" the word for "the same as" or only "looks like"? What is the Igbo for "the same"?
4. **Different**: NK "dị iche" showed "unique", not "different". Is "dị iche" correct for "different", or is another word used?
5. **Better / worse**: "ka mma" and "ka njọ" are constructed. What do speakers say for "better" and "worse"?
6. **Which is better?**: is "Kedụ nke ka mma?" natural? Or is "Nke nke ka mma?" more usual?
7. **Older / younger**: "okenye" = elder ✅. What is the normal word for "younger" (younger sibling / younger person) in standard Igbo? "nta" (younger one) appears on NK only in slang.
