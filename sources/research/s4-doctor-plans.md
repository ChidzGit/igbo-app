### Mini-dialogue (doctor)

Adapted from the igboguide "To the Hospital" conversation and Nkọwa examples. Spelling is standard Igbo with dots.

1. **A:** Ahụ adịghị m mma. Achọrọ m ịga hụ dibịa oyibo. *(I don't feel well. I want to go and see a doctor.)* [igboguide; app]
2. **B:** Ka anyị gaa ebe ahụ. *(Let us go there.)* [igboguide]
3. **A:** Enwere m isiọwụwa. *(I have a headache.)* [Nkọwa ex.]
4. **B:** Biko, nọdụ ala ebe a. *(Please sit down here.)* [igboguide, spelling converted from older text; check]
5. **A:** Aṅụrụ m ọgwụ isiọwụwa. *(I took medicine for headache.)* [Nkọwa ex.]
6. **B:** Gbakee ngwa ngwa. *(Get well soon.)* [Nkọwa, app]

### Culture notes (doctor)

- Two kinds of healer. A **dibịa** is a traditional healer or herbalist, and many people still use one. A hospital doctor is usually called **dibịa oyibo** in the older sources. Learners should know that the word for "doctor" changes with the setting. See Doubt 1.
- **Ndo** is said to someone who is ill or hurt, as a sign of sympathy (Wikivoyage: "used to console someone whenever something bad happens"). It is the first word to say at a sickbed.
- Wishing recovery with **Gbakee ngwa ngwa** is a normal, friendly phrase.
- Hospitals are called **ụlọ ọgwụ** (medicine house), and the name shows that "medicine" is part of the word. Nkọwa's headword is "ụnọ ọgwụ", so both forms may be heard.

### Doubts for the parent (doctor)

1. **"Doctor" word.** Nkọwa has no entry for **dọkịta** (checked as "dokita" too). Its only confirmed meaning is **dibịa** = traditional healer. The older sources say **dibịa oyibo** for a Western-style doctor. Which word do you use today for a hospital doctor: dọkịta, dibịa oyibo, or something else? This matches QUESTIONS-FOR-PARENT L31 and L35.
2. **"I have a headache".** The app teaches **"Isi na-awa m"**, but no saved source confirms "na-awa". Nkọwa gives **"Enwere m isiọwụwa"** (I have a headache). Which do people actually say? Is "isi ọwụwa" (two words, Wikivoyage) the usual spelling?
3. **Stomach ache.** Wikivoyage gives **afọ ohihi**, but Nkọwa has no "ohihi". The app's **"Afọ na-eme m"** is marked unsure with no source. What do people say?
4. **Needle or syringe.** Wikivoyage gives **ntụtụ** = needle and **ndụdụ** = syringe. Nkọwa gives **ntụtụ** = syringe (and its example mentions injections). Which is right for "injection"?
5. **Fever.** Nkọwa gives **ịba** = malaria or fever. Wikivoyage also uses ịba for flu. Is **ahụ ọkụ** the everyday word for fever, or is ịba the normal word?
6. **Hospital spelling.** Nkọwa's headword is **ụnọ ọgwụ**. Is **ụlọ ọgwụ** (used in the app and by igboguide) the spelling you would use?
7. **Gaps with no source (not added).** "Where does it hurt?", "Take this medicine twice a day", "I hope you feel better", and "Take care of your health" have no saved source. Please give the words, or we leave them out.

### Culture notes (plans and calls)

- Time is often given by the market day or by the clock. Igboguide's conversation says "at half past four" (ojiri ọkara gafee elekere anọ). Market-day names (Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ) are used for planning, and Nkọwa lists "Afọ" as the third market day (Wikipedia, "Igbo calendar"). This means the market day may be used as a date.
- Weekday names are borrowed from English (Mọnde, Tuuzde, Fraịde, Satọde). The traditional week has four market days, so learners will hear both systems.
- When someone is sorry for a problem, **Ndo** is used even on the phone. Wikivoyage says it is used to console someone when something bad happens.

### Doubts for the parent (plans and calls)

8. **Sunday.** The app uses **Ụbọchị Ụka** (lit. "church day"). Nkọwa's "ụka" does not mean church. It means speech, sour, or upright. Wikivoyage gives **Ụbọchị úkà** for Sunday and **ụlọ ụka** for church. Which word is correct: Ụbọchị Ụka, Ụbọchị úkà, or another?
9. **Wednesday and Thursday.** Nkọwa has Mọnde, Tuuzde, Fraịde and Satọde, but not Wensde or Tọsdọe. Wikivoyage has both. Should we teach them, and what spelling?
10. **"Call" (kpọọ).** Nkọwa's "kpọ" entry, on its first page, gives "hit / strike" and "take someone along". It does not show "call" yet. Wikivoyage and ilanguages use kpọọ for "call". Can you confirm that kpọọ = call?
11. **"Call you back".** Nkọwa has no entry for **kpọghachi**. Wikivoyage gives "Kpọghachi m" (call me back). Do people say "kpọghachi" or another phrase?
12. **"Wait".** Wikivoyage gives **chère** (wait) and **chetụ obere** (wait a bit). Nkọwa's "chere" is "think". Which verb means "wait" in ordinary use?
13. **Phone greeting and "Who is speaking?"** No saved source gives "Hello?" on the phone or "Who is speaking?". I have not added a line for them. What would you say? (For the record: "Ndewo" is the usual greeting, already in the app.)
14. **Constructed lines.** These are built from verified words but not copied from any source: "Ị ga-abịa?" (will you come?), "Bịa n'ụlọ m" (come to my house), "Enwere m oge" (I'm free), "Enweghị m oge" (I'm busy), "Gịnị ka ị na-eme echi?"
