## Culture notes

- **Proverbs signal belonging.** S3 says that using proverbs is a way to show you are "a true son or daughter of the soil" and to impress elders. Learners who use one correctly in front of elders will be well received.
- **Equal voice in the community.** S1 explains that in Igbo tradition, wealth or rank does not give extra rights. Everyone can speak at village meetings and use the village square. This is the context of "Egbe bere, ugo bere".
- **Proverbs are for elders and for explaining things.** S2's "Ilu bụ mmanụ…" compares proverbs to palm oil that makes words easier to eat, and S2's elder-proverb suggests that older people see more. A learner should not use proverbs with a child or peer as if they were ordinary sentences; they are most often used in serious talk.
- **Kola is an offer of welcome.** The saying "Onye wetara ọjị, wetara ndụ" (S2) connects bringing kola with bringing life. The custom of offering kola to a guest is widely known in Igboland, but I did not find it stated in the fetched sources; parent to check.

## Doubts for the parent

1. **"Ịtụ ilu" as "to say a proverb":** Nkọwa does not list this sense for tụ. The correct phrase for "to use or say a proverb" is unconfirmed; please check with a fluent speaker before teaching it.
2. **Spelling variants of the classic proverbs:** "Egbe bere, ugo bere…" appears as "Ebge bere" (S1) and "Egbe bere" (S2). Which is standard?
3. **Translations disagree:** "Ewu nwa ogbenye bụ chi ya" is "a poor man's goat is his cow" (S6) but "treated like a cow" (S3). "Onye fee eze…" is also rendered differently in two sources.
4. **Single-source items:** 12 of the 20 proverbs come from one source only (S2 or S3). Eight have two sources (one of them the S6 snippet, whose site is unnamed). S2 is a low-reliability site. Please check "Onye wetara ọjị, wetara ndụ" and "Igwe bụ ike" first.
5. **Your examples not found:** "Nwata kwọchaa aka…" (only "Nwata kwocha aka…" in S3); "Otu onye anaghị aka…" and "Ihe a na-eme n'abalị…" and "Ọ bụ ka ha na-eme…" were searched and not found in any source. Please send the full text or where you saw them.
6. **Tone marks:** Nkọwa shows tone marks on many words (for example ílú, ézè, ìgwè). The sources use no tone marks. The brief says to include tone only if a source gives it, so I did not add tones to proverbs.
