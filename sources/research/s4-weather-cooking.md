### Culture notes (weather)
- The Igbo homeland has two seasons: the rainy season (`udu mmiri`, WV) and the dry season (`ọkọchị`). WV: "There are only two seasons in the Igbo homeland". WV calls harmattan "a dusty trade wind that blows throughout West Africa"; NO example: "wind blows more in harmattan than dry season".
- Dry season (`ọkọchị`) is roughly late September to April on NO. Harmattan falls in that period.
- Farming follows the seasons. The planting and harvest months in the Igbo calendar tie to rain (see the s3 and calendar sources; not repeated here).
- Greeting about weather is common in IG: "Olee maka ụbọchị taa?" (How is the weather today?).

### Doubts for the parent (weather)
1. Rainy season: WV gives "udu mmiri" (Ùdù ḿmírí). NO returns `ùdù` = "pot / clay pot" and "udu" also means a clay instrument, not the season. Is "udu mmiri" the common word, or is the phrase used only in some areas?
2. Rain: is `mmiri` (NO sense "rain") the everyday word? NO's `mmiriozuzo` is "amount of rain on one occasion". Which is taught?
3. Hot: `ọkụ` is taught as "fire" in the curriculum. Is `ọkụ` also "hot" in everyday speech, or should "heat" be `okpomọkụ` (NO: "heat")? WV uses ọkụ for "hot" and IG uses "ökü" for hot.
4. Sun shining: which verb means "to shine"? `acha` is not on NO as a standalone word. Is "anwụ na-acha" right?
5. Thunder: NO gives `égbè` only as "kite (bird)". What is the everyday word for thunder?
6. Lightning: NO has `àmụma mmiri` (lightning). The BRIEF spelling `àmùmà` is "prophecy" on NO. Confirm the spelling.
7. Umbrella: is `nche anwụ` correct? It appears only in IG (non-standard orthography). NO `nche` = "guard / alertness".
8. Cloud: NO `urukpu` = "clouds" (plural?). Is there a singular cloud word, and is `igwe ojii` used for a black cloud or storm?
9. Source typos: IG "nnuku ikuke" (wind) probably means `nnukwu ikuke`; not verified.

### Culture notes (cooking)
- Food is eaten with soup. A starch (akpụ/fufu, garị, abacha) is taken with soup like ofe onugbu or ofe nsala. NO says `garị` is "swallowed" (A na-elo garị elo).
- White soup (`ofe nsala`) is usually cooked without oil (NO). Bitterleaf soup is named for the bitter leaf `onugbu`.
- Thanks for a meal: "Imeela" (WV: "you've done it") is used when someone brings you a meal. "Daalu" is the most polite "thank you" (WV).
- Calling someone who works in the kitchen: "Jisike" (use strength) is used to show support for hard work (WV). Use with an honorific.

### Doubts for the parent (cooking)
1. Cooking verbs: NO confirms `sie` (cook/boil). Which words are used for "fry", "stir", "pound", "cut" and "taste"? The BRIEF terms `ghee`, `kwoo`, `tụọ`, `bee`, `detụ ire` did not match on NO.
2. `esi nri`: NO's `esi` = sperm. Is "sie nri" the correct everyday phrase for "cook food"?
3. Pounded yam: the BRIEF asked for "pounded yam" but no source gives a word. Which word do you use?
4. Dishes: `okpa` and `moi moi` are not on NO. Are they used in Igbo, or are they names from another language? `egusi` is also not on NO as a standalone word.
5. Ofe ọha: NO's food sense was not captured in full. Confirm the dish name.
6. Wash: `sachaa` (NO: rinse / fade) vs the everyday word for washing food or hands.
7. `mmanụ nri` as "cooking oil": is the phrase used, or only `mmanụ`?
