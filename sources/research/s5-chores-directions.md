### Mini-dialogue (chores and help)

1. **A:** Ị ga enyere m aka? (Can you help me?) [IL]
2. **B:** Ee, m ga enyere gị aka. (Yes, I can help you.) [IL]
3. **A:** Biko, sa efere ndị a. (Please wash these plates.) [WV parts; CON sentence]
4. **B:** Ọ bụ onye ọzọ ga-eme? (Is it someone else's turn to do it?) [CON]
5. **A:** Nne m gwara m ka m hicha ụlọ. (My mother asked me to clean the house.) [CON, uses checked "hicha"]

### Culture notes

- Family yards (**ezi**) are often shared by an extended family. Chores such as sweeping and washing are usually done by the household, and children are expected to help. *(General knowledge, not from a saved page.)*
- Asking for help or a drink is polite with **Biko** (please): "Biko, nye m mmiri." [WV, IG]
- The same word **sa** (wash) is used for the body and for dishes, so context shows what is washed. [NKO]
- Dialect varies. IG uses older spelling and dialect forms (e.g. "ikuta" for fetch), so check forms before teaching them. [IG]

### Mini-dialogue (directions)

1. **A:** Olee ụzọ m ga-esi aga ahịa? (Which way do I go to the market?) [CON, parts checked]
2. **B:** Gbá na áká nri, gaa n'ihu. (Turn right, go straight ahead.) [WV, EX]
3. **A:** Ọ dị nso? (Is it near?) [EX]
4. **B:** Ee, ọ dị ezigbo nso. Gafee ụzọ a, ahịa ahụ dị n'aka ekpe. (Yes, it is very near. Cross this road, the market is on the left.) [IG for "ezigbo nso" pattern; "aka ekpe" WV/NKO; "gafee" NKO; sentence CON]
5. **A:** Kwụsị ebe a, biko. (Stop here, please.) [WV, EX]

### Culture notes

- Directions in Igbo often use the hands: **aka ekpe** (left hand) and **aka nri** (right hand, the eating hand). This is confirmed for left by NKO. [NKO, WV]
- Landmarks such as the church, the market and the road are common for directions. Describe "in front of" (n'ihu) or "near" (nso) rather than exact distances. [WV, EX]
- Dialect differences are large. IG and IL use older spellings and some different words. Give learners one form at a time and say it is one dialect. [IG, IL]
- Expect a "Biko" (please) when asking for directions or telling a driver to stop. [WV "Biko, buga m ebe ahụ", EX]

## Doubts for the parent

1. **Wash clothes**: the brief suggested "sụ akwa", but NKO gives sụ as "to have a shoe or garment on" (to wear). It does not mean wash. Use "sa uwe" only after checking.
2. **Sweep**: no sweep verb was found. "za" on NKO means answer, and "za ụlọ" is unsupported. Which verb do you want to teach for sweep?
3. **Iron clothes**: "ịwa" on NKO means split or break. No ironing word was found in any source.
4. **Left and right conflict**: WV gives aka nri = right and aka ekpe = left (matches NKO for left). IL gives "Turn left: ba na aka-nri" and "Turn right: ba na aka-ekpe", which is reversed. WV plus NKO agree, so IL looks wrong. Please confirm with a native speaker.
5. **Turn**: "tụgharịa" is not on NKO. The page for "tegharị" loaded with no entries. Check again.
6. **Junction**: "nkwụsị" is not on NKO and WV uses it for bus stop. The only junction word found is "ábọ" (WV). Which should we use?
7. **Bridge, roundabout, opposite, follow, rubbish, whose turn**: no confirmed Igbo word for bridge, opposite, follow this road, rubbish, or "whose turn". "okirikiri" is only "round" in WV.
8. **Sweep compound sentence**: a web-search summary claimed NKO has "Onye na-ezi ezi taa?" The fetched NKO page does not show it. Do not use without a check.
9. **Fetch water**: NKO "kụ" has no fetch-water sense. The only source is IG's dialect form "ikuta". Which form should the app use?
10. **Spelling**: NKO spells "hicha" (not "hichaa"). Confirm the form to teach.
11. **Tidy / arrange (hazie)**: not checked. Not in any local source.
