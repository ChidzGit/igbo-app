### Mini-dialogue (injuries)

1. **A:** Ihe mberede mere! *(An accident happened!)* [built from "ihe mberede"; "mere" not checked]
2. **B:** Ọ dị gị mgbu? *(Are you in pain?)* [built; candidate]
3. **A:** A gbajiri m ụkwụ m. *(I broke my leg.)* [Nkọwa example, under "gbajiri"]
4. **B:** Ndo. Kpọọ ambulans! *(Sorry. Call an ambulance!)* ["Ndo" Nkọwa ✅; "kpọọ ambulans" IL]
5. **A:** Apa ahụ bụ ebe o nwere ọnya. *(That scar is where the wound was.)* [Nkọwa example, under "ọnya"; this line is a memory, so it is a useful B1 link]

### Culture notes (injuries)

- **Ndo** is said to someone who is hurt or ill, as sympathy, not only as an apology. Saying it first is the normal response (Nkọwa; Wikivoyage; FO).
- Two kinds of care are normal: a **dibịa** (traditional healer) and a **clinic or hospital** (ụlọ ọrịa / ụlọ ọgwụ). See the Section 4 doctor unit.
- Ambulances are not common in many rural areas. Sources describe a person going to a clinic or a healer first. (Not verified from a source in this session; treat as a general note and ask the parent.)

### Mini-dialogue (relationships)

1. **A:** Enyi m, kedụ ka ị mere? *(My friend, how are you?)* [IG; in app]
2. **B:** Biko, gbaghara m. *(Please forgive me.)* [Nkọwa example]
3. **A:** M gbaghara gị. *(I forgive you.)* [built from "gbaghara" ✅; the grammar is not checked]
4. **B:** Anyị bụ enyi ruo afọ iri. *(We have been friends for ten years.)* [built candidate; see D21]
5. **A:** A hụrụ m gị n'anya. *(I love you.)* [UP]
6. **B:** Achọrọ m ịlụ gị. *(I want to marry you.)* [Nkọwa example]

### Culture notes (relationships)

- **Ndo** is used for both sorry and sympathy. Saying "sị ya ndo" (say sorry to him) is the normal way to ask someone to apologise on your behalf (Nkọwa example).
- **Enyi** (friend) covers people you share mutual affection with, even if they are not family (Nkọwa). In Igbo culture, friendship sits alongside family ties; the Ideal journal snippet says friendship is valued as much as blood ties (WS, not opened).
- **Marriage:** a WS snippet says marriages were often arranged to join families. This was not opened or checked, so treat it as unconfirmed.
- Be polite when apologising or asking for forgiveness: "Biko" (please) comes before "gbaghara m" in the Nkọwa example.

## Doubts for the parent

- **D1. ihe mberede or iheike?** Wikivoyage uses "ihe mberede" for accident, but Nkọwa's headword is "iheike" with a different tone. Which spelling should the app use?
- **D2. Fall: daa or dà?** Nkọwa headword is "dà" (fall). "dá" (high tone) means hit. The app's "daa" needs the right tone.
- **D3. Cut and injury.** No Igbo verb for "cut" was found. "bee" in Nkọwa is the insect bee, not a verb. "mmerụ" is not a headword in Nkọwa. What is the normal verb for "cut" and the word for "injury"?
- **D4. Burn.** Nothing confirmed for "I was burnt" (ọkụ gbara m). Is this the right form?
- **D5. Bleeding.** "ọbara na-agba" is not confirmed. What is the normal phrase for "bleed"?
- **D6. Bandage and plaster.** Only Wikivoyage. "ihe mmachi" is not in Nkọwa. Does a native speaker use "bandeeji" or another word?
- **D7. Jealousy in the app.** The app's doc-sick and body notes say "mgbu" means jealousy. Nkọwa's "ekworo" is the confirmed jealousy noun. An earlier Section 4 note says Nkọwa has a second "mgbu" entry for jealousy. This session I did not re-check "mgbu". Please confirm before the note stays.
- **D8. "Be careful!"** Nkọwa has only "kpacha" = scrape; make neat. "Kpachara anya" is not confirmed. What do people say to mean "be careful"?
- **D9. "Are you hurt?"** Built from "mgbu" and "dị". Not in any source. Confirm the usual question.
- **D10. Ambulance and help.** Sources differ: Wikivoyage "ụgbọ ihe mberede" (ambulance) vs IL "ambulans" (loanword). For help: Wikivoyage "Nyére m aka!" vs IL "enyemaka". Which do people use?
- **D11. Motorbike.** The sentence "Ọ dara site na moto" is built. Nkọwa's "moto" means car. Is there a separate motorbike word (for example "okada" or "ọkada")? Not checked.
- **D12. Swollen and first aid.** No Igbo word or phrase was found in any source.
- **D13. Friendship.** "ọbụbụenyi" is not in Nkọwa. What is the common noun for friendship?
- **D14. "I love you".** UP gives "A hụrụ m gị n'anya" and "A fụlụ m gị n'anya". Both are on the list. Which is more common? Nkọwa checked "ịhụnanya" (love) but not the full phrase.
- **D15. "I miss you".** "Obi gị na-eme m" comes from one source. Literal sense: "your heart is doing me". Check if this is right.
- **D16. "Engaged".** No word found. "nkwa" means promise, not engagement. Is there a standard phrase?
- **D17. Trust.** "ntụkwasị obi" is not in Nkọwa. What is the usual word?
- **D18. Visit.** The brief says "leta" = visit. Nkọwa's "letà" is only "letter". Is there a verb "visit" in another form?
- **D19. Jealousy verb.** "kwò" appears only on the "ekworo" entry page. Please confirm it is "be jealous".
- **D20. Quarrel.** "ịlụ ọgụ" is built from "lụ" (fight; strive). "ese okwu" is not checked. Which phrase do people use for "argue"?
- **D21. "Friends for ten years".** Built from "enyi", "afọ" and "iri". "iri" (ten) was not re-checked in this session.
- **D22. Apologise.** No separate verb for "apologise" was confirmed. "ndo" and "sị ya ndo" cover the meaning. Is that enough?

## Dialect note

Most Nkọwa entries used here are tagged "Standard Igbo". No Owerri or Onitsha variants were checked. The "ihe mberede" / "iheike" and "dà" / "dá" tone issues may differ by dialect.
