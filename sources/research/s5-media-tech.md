## Source ids

- **NO** = Nkọwa okwu dictionary, https://nkowaokwu.com/search?word=<word> (checked in a browser on 2026-11-10; the word is in the URL)
- **STYLE** = GitHub-hosted Igbo style guide that appears to follow Microsoft glossaries, https://www.github.com/bobreyes/styleguides/blob/master/docs/ig/README.md (community document, not an official Igbo body)
- **HF** = Igbo review dataset (Davlan/nollysenti), https://huggingface.co/datasets/Davlan/nollysenti/blob/main/ig/train.tsv (real user sentences, not dictionary examples)

- **NUST** = Obiorah, "From towncrier to radio: exploring the growth of newscasting in Igbo language" (2023), https://journals.nust.na/index.php/njlc/article/view/61
- **ANAM** = "The standard Igbo in radio broadcasting: a study of selected radio stations in Anambra state", https://nigerianjournalsonline.com/index.php/jollc/article/view/1358

Igbo Wikipedia (ig.wikipedia.org) was tried for technology terms. The two page addresses tried returned 404, and no Igbo Wikipedia page turned up in search. So **Igbo Wikipedia was not used**. Ask the parent to check it by hand if wanted.

Key for the check column: ✅ = exact entry on Nkọwa okwu. ⚠ = found, but the sense or form differs (noted). ❌ = not on Nkọwa okwu. – = not checked.

Coined vs borrowed: in each row, "coined" means a native Igbo word or compound. "borrowed" means an English word with Igbo spelling (STYLE uses the same pattern, e.g. Ịntanetị, Netwọk, weebụ).

### Culture notes

- Igbo radio news grew out of the old town criers. Community announcements were made by a person in the market or village, so radio news still feels like a public message ([NUST](https://journals.nust.na/index.php/njlc/article/view/61)).
- Radio newscasters in Anambra often speak with dialect in their Igbo. A study recommends standard Igbo training for them ([ANAM](https://nigerianjournalsonline.com/index.php/jollc/article/view/1358)). So a learner who learns standard Igbo may find local radio mixes in dialect.
- Igbo TV had limited airtime in one study (NTA Enugu). Dedicated Igbo stations exist, such as Zik FM and Igbo Radio online ([NUST](https://journals.nust.na/index.php/njlc/article/view/61)).

### Culture notes

- Igbo borrows English tech words and adapts them to Igbo sound rules. Diphthongs are not in Igbo, so "phone" can become "fonu" (UNIZIK abstract, search snippet) or "ekwentị" (a coined word on Nkọwa). Both forms exist in real speech.
- Microsoft-style guides ask writers to follow the rule: "Resist the temptation to borrow an English word as-is" and "create a term that a reasonably intelligent Igbo reader will understand" (STYLE). So coined words are preferred in formal writing, but borrowed words are common in speech.
- Igbo has an online dictionary and Igbo keyboards for smartphones, so typing Igbo on a phone is possible ([UNIZIK search snippet](https://journals.unizik.edu.ng/jmel/article/view/178)).
