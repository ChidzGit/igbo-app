### Culture notes

- Children are often named by gender or as "nwa", and older people may use the same word to address younger people. Wikivoyage says "nwá" (child) can sound patronising when used for an adult (WV). Learners should use a name or a respectful title.
- Remembering the dead is a yearly event: "ncheta" means an anniversary or a yearly remembrance (NKO ✅). Families mark a date every year, so memories of the past are often shared at these gatherings.
- Childhood stories are part of learning: the app's `beliefs` lesson says folk tales (akụkọ ifo) were told to children at night. That tip is not checked against a second source.
- Elders' experience is respected. Use "ọ bụ" and similar polite forms when you tell an older person about your past (WV honorifics section). Sentences here are plain forms; check tone with a native speaker.

### Doubts for the parent

1. **chefù or chefuo?** NKO headword is "chefù" with the example "ichefu". WV writes "Chéfùó". Which spelling should the app use?
2. **Experiential negative "I have never been to…"**: "Ahụbeghị m mbe mbụ" is confirmed on NKO, but "Agabeghị m…" (never been to) is not found in any source. Is "-beghị" correct for "go" too? Is there an affirmative experience form ("Agala m…" / "Ahụla m…")?
3. **Habitual past ("used to")**: NKO only gives the suffix "-karị" (habitually). Is "na-…karị" with a past "mgbe" clause the normal way to say "used to", or is there a separate past habitual form?
4. **"A long time ago"**: "n'oge ọkpụ" is confirmed on NKO as distant past. The app uses "n'oge gboo" and "mgbe ochie" (both unsure). Which phrase do speakers use?
5. **"Last year"**: WV gives "afọ gara aga". NKO only shows "afọ atọ gara aga" (three years ago). Confirm "afọ gara aga" = last year.
6. **Constructed sentences**: "chetara", "anụkarị", and "ruo ugbu a" are built from patterns, not from a source. Check before use.
7. **NKO did not load** for "ahụbeghị" and "agabeghị" as search words. The sentence "Ahụbeghị m mbe mbụ" was checked through the "mbe" entry instead.

### Culture notes

- Good wishes for the future are common. The igbofocus lesson gives "ihe oma diri" as the phrase for good luck or best wishes (IF). Learners can use it when a friend starts a new job or exam.
- A noble title given by the community is a respected goal for some families. NKO's "ọzọ" entry gives an example: "E chiri m ọzọ be nna m" (I was given the ọzọ title at my father's place), and the app's titles lesson (around line 2033 of data/curriculum.js) covers the title. Do not assume every family hopes for a title.
- Education is a common hope. The NKO example under "atụmatụ" is about an education plan. Sentences about school and jobs suit B1 learners.
- Talk about God is common when people speak about the future (the app's `beliefs` lesson). "Chukwu" is the high God in NKO's entry. The phrase "if God wills" is not in any checked source, so the parent should confirm how often it is said.

### Doubts for the parent

1. **olileanya or olịleanya?** The NKO page would not load. The one source (a proverb blog) spells it with a dot under "i". Which spelling is standard?
2. **atụmanya** (the brief's word for hope) was not checked: the NKO page would not load. It is not in the list above. Check it by hand on Nkọwa okwu if you want it added.
3. **Next year**: WV gives "afọ n'abịa abịa". The brief suggests "afọ ọzọ" or "afọ na-abịa". NKO's "ọzọ" means "again / another", so "afọ ọzọ" is not confirmed as "next year". Which do speakers use?
4. **"I want to become"**: "achọrọ m ịbụ" (to be) or "achọrọ m ịghọ" (to become)? NKO gives "bụ" as "to be" and "ghọ" as "become", but the infinitive "ịbụ" and "ịghọ" are not listed.
5. **"If God wills"**: "Ọ bụrụ na Chukwu kwe" comes from the brief only. Is it used by Igbo speakers, and is "kwe" the right verb?
6. **"One day" (otu ụbọchị)**: no source confirms the phrase.
7. **Nightmare (nrọ ọjọọ)**: WV only. NKO did not check it.
8. **Hope / "I hope that"**: "olileanya" and "Olileanya m bụ na…" are not confirmed by any dictionary. Do not teach until the parent confirms.
9. **Constructed sentences** (items marked ⚠) use checked words in new patterns: "achọrọ m ịbụ dọkịta", "ga-enwe", "ịmụ". Check them before use.
