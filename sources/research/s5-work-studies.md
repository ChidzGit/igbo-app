### 1b. Mini-dialogue (uses items above; sources: NO, WV)

1. **A:** Ọrụ ahụ amasịghị m. *(I did not like that job. NO)*
2. **B:** Ọ dị mma. Ị chọrọ ịmalite azụmahịa nke gị? *(That's fine. Do you want to start your own business? Built sentence; "amalite" unchecked.)*
3. **A:** Ee. Ego dị mkpa. Uru bụ ihe m chọrọ. *(Yes. Money is important. Profit is what I want. Built; "uru" ✅ NO.)*
4. **B:** Ahịa dị ukwuu n'ụlọ ahịa ahụ. *(There is a big market at that shop. Built; "ụlọ ahịa" ⚠ on NO.)*
5. **A:** Ọ dị mma. Azụmahịa ya na Okey adabaghị. *(OK. His business with Okey did not work out. NO example.)*

### 1c. Culture notes

- Igbo traders (*ndị ahịa*) are a big part of daily life. Market days and trading families are normal, and "azụmahịa" (business) is a respected way to earn a living.
- Apprenticeship (learning a trade by working for an older master) is a common path in Igboland. The Igbo word for it is not confirmed here (see Doubts).
- "Ụlọọrụ" (office or company) is used for both a workplace and a business. Context decides which.
- Bargaining at the market is normal. Do not treat it as rude.

### 1d. Doubts for the parent

1. **Office:** Nkọwa writes *ụlọọrụ* (one word). Our brief had *ụlọ ọrụ* (two words). Which spelling should the app use? The app does not have this word yet.
2. **Salary:** WS gives *ugwuọnwa*, but Nkọwa does not have it. Is *ụgwọ ọrụ* ✅ the right word for the course, or is there a better term for "salary"?
3. **Loss:** *ọghọm* sense 3 = loss/failure, but its main sense is "disadvantage". *mfu* is not on Nkọwa. Which word should be used for "loss" (money or business)?
4. **Apprentice:** no confirmed Igbo word. Do we leave this out, or ask a native speaker for the usual term (*nwa boyi*, *ọzụzụ*, or other)?
5. **Interview:** no confirmed phrase. Which term should be used?
6. **To work:** Nkọwa's *rụọ* first sense is "soil/spoil/damage". The app already uses "Ị na-arụ ọrụ gịnị?" (What work do you do?) from job-talk. Is *rụ* used for "to work" in standard Igbo? The verb *ruo* (reach) is a different word.
7. **Hard work:** *rụsie ọrụ ike* is not on Nkọwa. Use *ọrụ ike* or ask the parent.

### 2b. Mini-dialogue (sources: NO, app)

1. **A:** Ule anyị ga-ebido n'izụụka ọzọ. *(Our exams will start next week. NO example.)*
2. **B:** Ana m amụ mgbakọ na mwepụ. *(I am learning addition and subtraction. NO example.)*
3. **A:** Ị na-agụ akwụkwọ ike? *(Are you studying hard? Built from *gụ* and *akwụkwọ* in app; not checked as a question.)*
4. **B:** Ee. Asambodo m ga-abụ ihe kacha mkpa. *(Yes. My certificate will be the most important thing. Built sentence; *asambodo* ✅ NO.)*
5. **A:** Ọ ga-abanye mahadum n'afọ na-abịa abịa. *(She will enter university next year. NO example, translation doubt.)*

### 2c. Culture notes

- Education is highly valued in Igbo families. Parents often pay large costs to send children to school or university.
- Exam results and certificates (*asambodo*) are shown with pride at family events.
- Students are often expected to help at home as well as study. This is normal.
- Using a full title (for example *onyenkụzi* or *dọkịta*) is a sign of respect.

### 2d. Doubts for the parent

1. **Pass / fail:** *gafee* and *dara* are NOT confirmed for exam sense on Nkọwa (only other senses). Which verbs should the app use for "pass" and "fail"?
2. **Result:** *nsonaazụ* means "suffix" on Nkọwa. Do we use a different word, or leave out "result"?
3. **Degree:** *digrii* is not on Nkọwa. Is it used as an English loanword, or is there an Igbo term?
4. **Graduate:** *gụchaa* not found. Which term should be used?
5. **Medicine:** *ọgwụ* is "stump" on Nkọwa's first sense. Is "medicine" a later sense, or a different word (for example, *ọgwụ ọgwụ*)?
6. **Mahadum translation:** Nkọwa translates "Ọ ga-abanye mahadum n'afọ na-abịa abịa" as "next month", but the literal reading is "next year". Which is right?
7. **Homophones:** *amụ* (noun, penis on Nkọwa) and *imụ* (verb, to learn). Keep only *imụ* in the app. Confirm that *na-amụ* is used for study.
